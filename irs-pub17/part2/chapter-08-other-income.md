## Introduction

This chapter discusses many kinds of income other than wages, salaries, interest, and dividends. In general, all income is taxable unless specifically excluded by law.

---

## Prizes, Awards, and Gambling Winnings

### Prizes and Awards

**General Rule:** All prizes and awards are taxable.

This includes:
- Lottery winnings
- Raffle prizes
- Contest winnings
- Door prizes
- TV game show prizes
- Awards from employers (unless qualifying achievement awards)

### Non-Cash Prizes

If you receive merchandise or property as a prize, you must include its **fair market value** as income.

### Exceptions to Taxation

Prizes and awards may be excluded if ALL of these apply:
1. You were selected without any action on your part
2. You are not required to perform substantial future services
3. The prize is transferred directly to a charity or governmental unit

### Gambling Winnings

**All gambling winnings are taxable**, including:
- Casino games
- Lotteries
- Raffles
- Horse racing
- Sports betting
- Online gambling

**Reporting:**
- **Form W-2G:** Payer reports to IRS if winnings exceed certain thresholds
- Report all winnings on Schedule 1, line 8b

**Gambling Losses:**
- Deductible only if you itemize
- Limited to the amount of gambling winnings
- Keep detailed records (date, type, location, winnings/losses)

## Bartering

### What Is Bartering?

Bartering is the exchange of property or services without using money.

**Examples:**
- A plumber fixes a dentist's pipes in exchange for dental work
- Trading vehicles with a neighbor
- Exchanging professional services

### Tax Treatment

You must include the fair market value of property or services received in income.

**Barter Exchanges:**
- Form 1099-B may be issued for exchange transactions
- Report on Schedule C if related to your business

## Canceled Debts

### General Rule

If a debt you owe is canceled or forgiven for less than the amount you owe, you generally must include the canceled amount in income.

**Form 1099-C:** Creditors must report cancellation of debt of $600 or more.

### Exceptions

Canceled debt is NOT taxable if:

| Exception | Situation |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Bankruptcy** | Debt canceled in a Title 11 bankruptcy case |
| **Insolvency** | You were insolvent immediately before cancellation |
| **Qualified farm debt** | Certain farm debts canceled |
| **Qualified real property business debt** | Certain business real property debt |
| **Qualified principal residence debt** | Mortgage debt on main home (through 2025) |
| **Student loans** | Certain qualifying loan forgiveness programs |

### Insolvency

You are insolvent if your total liabilities exceed the fair market value of your total assets immediately before the cancellation.

**Use Form 982** to report exclusions and reduce tax attributes.

### Mortgage Debt Forgiveness

Qualified principal residence indebtedness forgiven may be excluded from income:
- Maximum exclusion: $750,000 ($375,000 MFS)
- Applies to debt used to buy, build, or improve main home
- Extended through 2025

## Rental Income

### What Is Rental Income?

Rental income includes:
- Rent payments
- Advance rent
- Security deposits (if not refunded)
- Payments for canceling a lease
- Property or services received as rent (FMV)

### Reporting

Report rental income and expenses on **Schedule E**.

### Rental Expenses

Common deductible expenses:
- Mortgage interest
- Property taxes
- Insurance
- Repairs and maintenance
- Depreciation
- Utilities (if paid by landlord)
- Property management fees
- Advertising
- Legal and professional fees

### Special Rules

**Personal Use:**
If you use the property personally for more than the greater of:
- 14 days, or
- 10% of the days it was rented

Your deductions may be limited.

**Not Rented for Profit:**
If you don't rent for profit, you can deduct expenses only up to the amount of rental income.

See Pub. 527, Residential Rental Property, for more information.

## Royalties

### What Are Royalties?

Royalties are payments for the use of property, including:
- Oil, gas, and mineral rights
- Patents
- Copyrights
- Trademarks
- Franchises

### Reporting

| Type | Where to Report |
|------|-----------------|
| Oil, gas, minerals | Schedule E, Part I |
| Patents, copyrights (not your trade/business) | Schedule E, Part I |
| Self-employment royalties | Schedule C |

### Oil and Gas Royalties

If you receive oil or gas royalties, you may be able to claim a **depletion deduction**. Use percentage depletion or cost depletion (whichever is greater).

## Social Security Benefits

### Taxability of Benefits

Your Social Security benefits may be taxable depending on your income level.

**Up to 50% may be taxable if:**
Combined income* is between:
- $25,000 - $34,000 (Single, HOH, QSS)
- $32,000 - $44,000 (MFJ)

**Up to 85% may be taxable if:**
Combined income* exceeds:
- $34,000 (Single, HOH, QSS)
- $44,000 (MFJ)

*Combined income = AGI + Nontaxable interest + 1/2 of Social Security benefits

**Married Filing Separately:** If you lived with your spouse at any time during the year, up to 85% of your benefits may be taxable.

### Calculation Worksheet

Use the worksheet in the Form 1040 instructions or Pub. 915 to calculate the taxable portion.

### Form SSA-1099

You'll receive Form SSA-1099 showing total benefits paid in Box 5.

## Unemployment Compensation

### General Rule

Unemployment compensation is **fully taxable**.

This includes:
- State unemployment benefits
- Federal unemployment benefits
- Railroad unemployment benefits
- Disability benefits paid as substitute for unemployment

### Reporting

- **Form 1099-G:** Shows unemployment compensation received
- Report on Schedule 1, line 7

### Withholding

You can choose to have federal income tax withheld from unemployment benefits by filing **Form W-4V**.

## Digital Assets

### What Are Digital Assets?

Digital assets include:
- Cryptocurrency (Bitcoin, Ethereum, etc.)
- Non-fungible tokens (NFTs)
- Stablecoins
- Other virtual currencies

### Tax Treatment

Digital assets are treated as **property** for tax purposes.

| Transaction | Tax Treatment |
|-------------|---------------|
| Sale or exchange | Capital gain/loss |
| Received as payment | Ordinary income (FMV) |
| Mining | Ordinary income (FMV when received) |
| Staking rewards | Ordinary income (FMV when received) |
| Airdrop | Ordinary income (FMV when received) |
| Hard fork | May be income depending on circumstances |

### Reporting Requirements

**Form 1040 Question:** You must answer the digital asset question on page 1.

**Answer "Yes" if you:**
- Received digital assets as payment for goods or services
- Transferred digital assets for free
- Sold, exchanged, or disposed of digital assets
- Had a financial interest in digital assets

**Answer "No" if:**
- You only held digital assets and had no transactions
- You transferred digital assets between your own wallets

### Form 1099-DA (New for 2025)

Brokers may send Form 1099-DA for digital asset sales. Even if received, you must still answer the digital asset question.

### Record Keeping

Maintain detailed records of:
- Date acquired
- Date sold
- Purchase price (basis)
- Sale price
- Transaction fees
- Wallet addresses

## Other Items

### Alimony

| Divorce Date | Tax Treatment |
|--------------|---------------|
| Before 2019 | Taxable to recipient, deductible by payer |
| 2019 or later | Not taxable; not deductible |

### Life Insurance Proceeds

- Generally **not taxable** when received due to death
- **Taxable** if received in installments (interest portion)
- **Taxable** if sold policy for cash

### Scholarships and Fellowships

- **Tax-free** if used for tuition and required fees, books, supplies, equipment
- **Taxable** if used for room and board, travel, optional equipment

### Jury Duty Pay

- Taxable income
- If you give jury pay to your employer because they paid your salary during jury duty, you can deduct it on Schedule 1

### Found Property

If you find property, you must report its fair market value as income.

### Illegal Income

Income from illegal activities is taxable (bribes, drug sales, etc.).

### Hobby Income

Income from hobbies is taxable. Hobby expenses are not deductible.

### Punitive Damages

Generally taxable (except certain wrongful death cases).

---

## Items NOT Taxable

| Item | Exception |
|------|-----------|
| Life insurance death benefits | If received in installments, interest is taxable |
| Gifts and inheritances | Interest/gains after receipt are taxable |
| Workers' compensation | Fully excludable |
| Compensatory damages for physical injury | Punitive damages are taxable |
| Child support | Never taxable |
| Welfare benefits | Generally not taxable |
| Disaster relief payments | If from FEMA or qualified charity |

---

## How To Report Other Income

Most other income is reported on **Schedule 1**, Part I:
- Line 1: Taxable refunds of state taxes
- Line 2a: Alimony received (pre-2019 divorces)
- Line 3: Schedule C income
- Line 4: Other gains or losses
- Line 5: Rental income (Schedule E)
- Line 6: Farm income
- Line 7: Unemployment compensation
- Line 8: Other income (specify)
