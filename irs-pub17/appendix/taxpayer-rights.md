## The Taxpayer Bill of Rights

The IRS adopted a Taxpayer Bill of Rights in 2014, which groups existing rights into 10 fundamental rights.

---

## 1. The Right to Be Informed

Taxpayers have the right to know:
- What they need to do to comply with tax laws
- Clear explanations of the laws and IRS procedures
- Information about IRS decisions affecting their accounts
- Clear explanations of outcomes

**This means:**
- You have the right to clear explanations of tax laws
- The IRS must provide understandable notices
- You're entitled to know why the IRS is asking for information

---

## 2. The Right to Quality Service

Taxpayers have the right to:
- Receive prompt, courteous, and professional assistance
- Be spoken to in a way they can understand
- Receive clear and easily understandable communications
- Speak to a supervisor about inadequate service

**This means:**
- IRS employees should treat you professionally
- You can request to speak with a supervisor
- The IRS should respond to your inquiries promptly

---

## 3. The Right to Pay No More than the Correct Amount of Tax

Taxpayers have the right to:
- Pay only the amount of tax legally due
- Have the IRS apply all tax payments properly

**This means:**
- You're entitled to claim all deductions and credits you qualify for
- The IRS should correctly apply your payments
- You can dispute assessments you believe are incorrect

---

## 4. The Right to Challenge the IRS's Position and Be Heard

Taxpayers have the right to:
- Raise objections
- Provide additional documentation in response to IRS actions
- Expect the IRS to consider their objections timely and fairly
- Receive a response if the IRS doesn't agree with their position

**This means:**
- You can disagree with the IRS
- You're entitled to present your case
- The IRS must consider your evidence

---

## 5. The Right to Appeal an IRS Decision in an Independent Forum

Taxpayers have the right to:
- A fair and impartial administrative appeal of most IRS decisions
- Receive a written response regarding the Office of Appeals' decision

**This means:**
- You can appeal disagreements to the IRS Office of Appeals
- Appeals officers are independent from examination
- You can take certain matters to Tax Court

---

## 6. The Right to Finality

Taxpayers have the right to know:
- The maximum amount of time to challenge the IRS's position
- The maximum amount of time the IRS has to audit a particular tax year
- When the IRS has finished an audit

**This means:**
- The IRS generally has 3 years to audit your return
- You generally have 3 years to claim a refund
- There should be a definite end to IRS examinations

---

## 7. The Right to Privacy

Taxpayers have the right to expect:
- IRS inquiries, examinations, and enforcement actions will comply with the law
- IRS will not intrude more than necessary
- IRS will respect all due process rights, including search and seizure protections

**This means:**
- The IRS can't share your tax information inappropriately
- Audits should be conducted professionally
- Your privacy rights are protected by law

---

## 8. The Right to Confidentiality

Taxpayers have the right to expect:
- Their tax information will be kept confidential
- The IRS won't disclose information except as authorized by law
- Appropriate action will be taken against employees who violate confidentiality

**This means:**
- Your tax return information is confidential
- The IRS can share information only as permitted by law
- IRS employees face penalties for unauthorized disclosures

---

## 9. The Right to Retain Representation

Taxpayers have the right to:
- Retain an authorized representative of their choice
- Seek assistance from a Low Income Taxpayer Clinic

**Authorized representatives include:**
- Attorneys
- Certified Public Accountants (CPAs)
- Enrolled Agents
- Others authorized to practice before the IRS

**This means:**
- You don't have to face the IRS alone
- Your representative can act on your behalf
- Low-income taxpayers have access to free help

---

## 10. The Right to a Fair and Just Tax System

Taxpayers have the right to expect:
- The tax system to consider facts and circumstances
- The IRS to consider the ability to pay
- Access to the Taxpayer Advocate Service for unresolved issues

**This means:**
- The IRS should apply the law fairly
- Hardship situations should be considered
- You have access to the Taxpayer Advocate if needed

---

## Examinations (Audits)

### Selection for Examination

Returns are selected for examination through:
- Computer scoring (DIF - Discriminant Information Function)
- Document matching (comparing returns to information reports)
- Related examinations (connection to other audited taxpayers)
- Random selection
- Local compliance projects

### By Mail

Most examinations are conducted by mail:
- IRS sends a letter explaining the issue
- You respond with requested documentation
- IRS reviews and provides results

### By Interview

Some examinations require an interview:
- At an IRS office, or
- At your home, business, or accountant's office
- You can have a representative present

### Repeat Examinations

The IRS generally won't examine the same items in successive years unless:
- There's a substantial change, or
- A significant issue was identified

You can request an audit reconsideration if you believe the examination was improper.

---

## Appeals

If you disagree with the examination results:

### Informal Appeals

1. **Request a conference** with the examiner's supervisor
2. **Provide additional information** that supports your position

### Formal Appeals

1. **Request Appeals** within 30 days of the examination report
2. **Submit a written protest** (if amount exceeds $25,000)
3. **Meet with Appeals Officer** who is independent from examination

### Tax Court

If you can't resolve through Appeals:
- You have 90 days to petition Tax Court after receiving Notice of Deficiency
- You can represent yourself or hire a representative
- Small tax case procedure available for amounts under $50,000

---

## Collections

If you owe tax you can't pay:

### Your Options

1. **Installment Agreement:** Pay over time
2. **Offer in Compromise:** Settle for less than owed
3. **Currently Not Collectible:** Temporary delay in collection
4. **Penalty Abatement:** Request removal of penalties

### IRS Collection Actions

The IRS can:
- File a federal tax lien
- Issue a levy (seizure of assets)
- Garnish wages
- Seize property

### Your Rights During Collection

- Receive notice before levies
- Request a Collection Due Process hearing
- Keep certain property exempt from levy
- Have collection actions reviewed

### Innocent Spouse Relief

If your spouse understated tax without your knowledge:
- You may not be responsible for the additional tax
- File Form 8857 to request relief
- Must file within 2 years of collection activity

### Potential Third-Party Contacts

The IRS must:
- Notify you before contacting third parties
- Provide periodic reports of contacts made
- Respect your privacy in communications

---

## Refunds

### Your Right to a Refund

- You're entitled to a refund of any overpayment
- You must claim refunds within 3 years of filing or 2 years of payment
- The IRS must pay interest on delayed refunds

### Refund Offsets

Your refund may be offset (reduced) for:
- Past-due federal taxes
- Past-due child support
- Federal agency debts
- State taxes

You'll receive notice if your refund is offset.

## Taxpayer Advocate Service (TAS)

### What Is TAS?

An independent organization within the IRS that helps taxpayers resolve problems they haven't been able to resolve through normal channels.

### When to Contact TAS

- You're experiencing economic harm or significant cost
- You've experienced a delay of more than 30 days to resolve your issue
- You haven't received a response by the date promised
- A system or procedure isn't working as intended

### How to Contact TAS

**Online:** [TaxpayerAdvocate.IRS.gov](https://www.taxpayeradvocate.irs.gov)

**Phone:** 1-877-777-4778

**In Person:** Every state has at least one TAS office

### What TAS Can Do

- Ensure your problem receives proper attention
- Expedite resolution of your issue
- Help you understand your rights
- Identify systemic problems affecting many taxpayers
