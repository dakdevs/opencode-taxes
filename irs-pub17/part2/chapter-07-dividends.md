## Introduction

Dividends are distributions of money, stock, or other property paid to you by a corporation or mutual fund. Most dividends are paid in cash, but some are paid in stock or other property.

**Form 1099-DIV:** You should receive Form 1099-DIV from each payer for distributions of $10 or more.

---

## Ordinary Dividends

### What Are Ordinary Dividends?

Ordinary dividends are the most common type of distribution from a corporation or mutual fund. They are paid out of the earnings and profits of a corporation.

**Report on:** Form 1040, line 3b

### Examples of Ordinary Dividends

- Cash dividends from domestic corporations
- Dividends from foreign corporations
- Mutual fund distributions from interest income
- Money market fund "dividends" (actually interest)
- Dividends from tax-exempt money market funds

---

## Qualified Dividends

### What Are Qualified Dividends?

Qualified dividends are ordinary dividends that qualify for the same lower tax rates as long-term capital gains.

### Tax Rates for Qualified Dividends (2025)

| Tax Bracket | Rate on Qualified Dividends |
|-------------|----------------------------|
| 10% or 12% bracket | 0% |
| 22%, 24%, 32%, 35% brackets | 15% |
| 37% bracket | 20% |

### Requirements for Qualified Dividends

To be qualified, dividends must meet these requirements:

**1. Paid by a U.S. corporation or qualified foreign corporation**

A qualified foreign corporation is one that:
- Is incorporated in a U.S. possession
- Is eligible for benefits of a U.S. tax treaty
- Has stock readily tradable on U.S. securities market

**2. Not specifically excluded**

Not qualified dividends:
- Capital gain distributions
- Dividends on deposits with savings institutions
- Dividends on stock held less than required period
- Dividends from tax-exempt organizations
- Dividends from mutual savings banks
- Dividends from farmers' cooperatives

**3. Holding period requirement met**

You must have held the stock for more than 60 days during the 121-day period that begins 60 days before the ex-dividend date.

For preferred stock dividends attributable to periods over 366 days, you must hold the stock for more than 90 days during the 181-day period.

### Form 1099-DIV Reporting

- **Box 1a:** Total ordinary dividends
- **Box 1b:** Qualified dividends (subset of Box 1a)

## Capital Gain Distributions

### What Are Capital Gain Distributions?

Capital gain distributions are payments from mutual funds or REITs that represent gains from the sale of securities or other assets held by the fund.

### Tax Treatment

Capital gain distributions are **always long-term** regardless of how long you've owned your shares.

**Report on:** Schedule D, Form 1040

### Form 1099-DIV Reporting

- **Box 2a:** Total capital gain distributions
- **Box 2b:** Unrecaptured Section 1250 gain (28% rate)
- **Box 2c:** Section 1202 gain
- **Box 2d:** Collectibles (28%) gain

---

## Nondividend Distributions

### Return of Capital

Nondividend distributions (return of capital) are distributions that are not paid out of earnings and profits. They are a return of your investment.

**Tax Treatment:**
1. Not taxable until they exceed your basis in the stock
2. Reduce your basis in the stock
3. After basis is reduced to zero, report as capital gain

### Form 1099-DIV Reporting

**Box 3:** Nondividend distributions

### Example

```
You bought 100 shares of XYZ Corp for $1,000 (basis = $1,000)
Year 1: Receive $100 nondividend distribution
        Taxable: $0
        New basis: $900

Year 2: Receive $200 nondividend distribution
        Taxable: $0
        New basis: $700

Year 3: Receive $800 nondividend distribution
        $700 reduces basis to $0
        $100 is taxable as capital gain
```

## Dividends on Insurance Policies

### General Rule

Dividends on life insurance policies are generally a partial return of premiums paid. They are not taxable unless they exceed the total premiums paid.

### When Dividends Are Taxable

| Situation | Treatment |
|-----------|-----------|
| Dividends less than premiums paid | Not taxable |
| Dividends exceed premiums paid | Excess is taxable |
| Dividends left with insurer to earn interest | Interest is taxable |
| Dividends used to buy paid-up additions | Generally not taxable |

## Stock Dividends and Stock Splits

### Nontaxable Stock Dividends

Stock dividends are generally not taxable if:
- They are distributions of additional shares of the same class
- All shareholders receive proportionally

When you receive nontaxable stock dividends:
- Allocate your original basis among all shares (old and new)
- Holding period includes the original shares' holding period

### Taxable Stock Dividends

Stock dividends are taxable if:
- You have a choice between stock or cash
- Distribution is disproportionate (some get stock, some get cash)
- Distribution is on preferred stock
- Distribution of common stock to some and preferred to others

---

## Dividends Received in Foreign Currency

If you receive dividends in foreign currency:
1. Convert to U.S. dollars using the exchange rate on the date received
2. Report in U.S. dollars
3. Any gain or loss from exchange rate changes when you convert is separate ordinary gain or loss

---

## Reinvested Dividends

If your dividends are automatically reinvested:
- Still taxable in the year of the distribution
- Increases your basis in the investment
- Keep records of all reinvested dividends for basis calculation

---

## Constructive Dividends

Constructive dividends occur when a corporation provides a benefit to a shareholder that is not characterized as a dividend but should be.

**Examples:**
- Below-market loans to shareholders
- Excessive salary to shareholder-employees
- Personal expenses paid by corporation
- Bargain purchases from corporation

---

## How To Report

### Form 1040 Reporting

| Type | Where to Report |
|------|-----------------|
| Ordinary dividends (total) | Line 3b |
| Qualified dividends | Line 3a |
| Capital gain distributions | Schedule D |
| Return of capital | Reduce basis; Form 8949 if gain |

### Schedule B Required If:

You must file Schedule B if:
- Your ordinary dividends exceed **$1,500**
- You received dividends as a nominee
- You have foreign accounts or trusts (Part III)

### Example: Reporting Dividends

**Form 1099-DIV shows:**
- Box 1a (Ordinary dividends): $2,000
- Box 1b (Qualified dividends): $1,500
- Box 2a (Capital gain distributions): $300
- Box 3 (Nondividend distributions): $100

**Report on Form 1040:**
- Line 3a (Qualified dividends): $1,500
- Line 3b (Ordinary dividends): $2,000
- Schedule D: $300 long-term capital gain
- Reduce stock basis by $100

## Special Situations

### Mutual Fund Distributions

Mutual funds make different types of distributions:

| Fund Distribution | Character |
|-------------------|-----------|
| Ordinary dividends | Ordinary income |
| Exempt-interest dividends | Tax-exempt (report on 1040, line 2a) |
| Capital gain distributions | Long-term capital gain |
| Return of capital | Nontaxable; reduces basis |

### REIT Distributions

Real Estate Investment Trust distributions may include:
- Ordinary dividends
- Qualified dividends
- Capital gain distributions
- Return of capital

### S Corporation Distributions

S corporation distributions are generally not taxable if you have sufficient stock basis. Report separately from regular dividends.

### Patronage Dividends

Dividends from cooperatives are generally:
- Ordinary income to the extent of allocated share of taxable income
- May be reported on Form 1099-PATR

---

## Backup Withholding

Banks and other payers must withhold 24% of dividends if:
- You don't provide your correct TIN
- IRS notifies the payer of incorrect TIN
- You underreported dividends

**Box 4** on Form 1099-DIV shows backup withholding. Report this on Form 1040 as tax paid.
