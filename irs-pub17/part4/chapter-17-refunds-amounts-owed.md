## Introduction

After you've calculated your total tax and credits, you need to determine whether you:
- Have an **overpayment** (refund due), or
- Have an **amount owed** (balance due)

---

## Figuring Your Overpayment or Amount Owed

### Basic Calculation

```
Total Tax (Line 24)                    $_______
Minus: Total Payments (Line 33)        $_______
                                       ─────────
Result:
If negative = Overpayment (refund)     $_______
If positive = Amount You Owe           $_______
```

### What Counts as Payments?

**Form 1040 Payments (Lines 25-32):**
- Federal income tax withheld (W-2, 1099)
- Estimated tax payments
- Amount applied from prior year return
- Earned Income Credit
- Additional Child Tax Credit
- American Opportunity Credit (refundable portion)
- Premium Tax Credit (net)
- Other refundable credits

## Refunds

### Ways to Receive Your Refund

**Direct Deposit (Recommended)**
- Fastest method
- Up to 3 accounts (use Form 8888)
- Checking, savings, or certain prepaid cards
- IRA accounts (use Form 8888)

**Paper Check**
- Mailed to address on return
- Takes longer
- Risk of lost/stolen check

### Split Your Refund

Use **Form 8888** to:
- Deposit into up to 3 accounts
- Buy U.S. savings bonds
- Split between accounts and bonds

### Refund Timing

| Method | Timeline |
|--------|----------|
| E-file with direct deposit | Usually within 21 days |
| E-file with paper check | 4 weeks or more |
| Paper return with direct deposit | 4-6 weeks |
| Paper return with paper check | 6-8 weeks |

### ACTC/EIC Refund Delay

Refunds claiming ACTC or EIC cannot be issued before **mid-February**.

### Check Refund Status

**Where's My Refund?**
- Online: [IRS.gov/Refunds](https://www.irs.gov/refunds)
- App: IRS2Go mobile app
- Phone: 1-800-829-1954

**Information needed:**
- Social Security Number
- Filing status
- Exact refund amount

### Apply Refund to Next Year

You can apply all or part of your overpayment to next year's estimated tax:
- Enter amount on Form 1040, line 36
- Cannot be changed after filing

### Offset of Refunds

Your refund may be reduced (offset) if you owe:
- Past-due federal taxes
- Past-due child support
- Past-due student loans
- State income taxes
- Other federal debts

You'll receive notice if your refund is offset.

## What If You Owe Tax

### Payment Due Date

Tax owed is due by **April 15, 2026** for tax year 2025.

> **Note:** Extensions to file do NOT extend the time to pay.

### Electronic Payment Options

**IRS Direct Pay**
- Free
- Pay from bank account
- [IRS.gov/DirectPay](https://www.irs.gov/directpay)

**EFTPS (Electronic Federal Tax Payment System)**
- Free
- Must enroll in advance
- [EFTPS.gov](https://www.eftps.gov)

**Credit/Debit Card**
- Processing fees apply
- Through approved providers

**Digital Wallet**
- PayPal, Click to Pay, etc.
- Fees may apply

### Same-Day Wire Transfer

Available through your bank. Contact your financial institution.

### Cash Payments

- At participating 7-Eleven, CVS, and other retail stores
- Through IRS payment partner
- Fees apply

### Check or Money Order

- Make payable to "United States Treasury"
- Include SSN, phone number, tax year, and form number
- Mail with Form 1040-V (payment voucher)

## Payment Options

### If You Can't Pay in Full

**Don't ignore the bill.** File your return even if you can't pay. Options include:

1. **Short-term payment plan** (up to 180 days)
2. **Installment agreement** (monthly payments)
3. **Offer in compromise**
4. **Temporary delay** (Currently Not Collectible)

### Apply for Payment Plan

**Online:**
- [IRS.gov/OPA](https://www.irs.gov/opa)
- Available 24/7
- Immediate approval for qualifying taxpayers

**By phone:** 1-800-829-1040

**By mail:** Form 9465

## Installment Agreements

### Types of Installment Agreements

| Type | Amount Owed | Terms |
|------|-------------|-------|
| **Guaranteed** | $10,000 or less | Must pay within 3 years |
| **Streamlined** | $50,000 or less | Must pay within 72 months |
| **Non-streamlined** | Over $50,000 | Requires financial disclosure |

### Guaranteed Installment Agreement

You **must** be approved if:
- You owe $10,000 or less (excluding penalties and interest)
- You've filed all required returns
- You haven't had an installment agreement in past 5 years
- You can pay within 3 years
- You agree to file and pay on time in the future

### Online Payment Agreement

Apply online if you owe $50,000 or less:
- Set up payment plan
- Revise existing agreement
- Check agreement status

### Setup Fees (2025)

| Agreement Type | Setup Fee |
|---------------|-----------|
| Direct debit (online) | $22 |
| Non-direct debit (online) | $69 |
| Phone/mail setup | $178 |
| Low-income (< 250% poverty) | $0 or reduced |

### User Fees

Additional monthly fees may apply.

### Form 9465

Use **Form 9465** to request an installment agreement by mail.

## Penalties and Interest

### Failure to File Penalty

- **5% of unpaid tax per month** (or part of month)
- Maximum: 25% of unpaid tax
- If more than 60 days late, minimum penalty is lesser of $510 or 100% of tax owed

### Failure to Pay Penalty

- **0.5% of unpaid tax per month**
- Maximum: 25% of unpaid tax
- Reduced to 0.25% if installment agreement in place

### Combined Penalties

If both penalties apply in same month, failure to file penalty is reduced by failure to pay amount.

### Interest

- Charged on unpaid tax and penalties
- Compounds daily
- Rate: Federal short-term rate + 3%
- Rate changes quarterly

### First-Time Penalty Abatement

You may qualify for penalty relief if:
- No penalties in prior 3 years
- Filed all required returns
- Paid (or arranged to pay) all tax due

### Reasonable Cause Relief

Penalties may be removed if you have reasonable cause:
- Death or serious illness
- Fire, casualty, or natural disaster
- Unable to obtain records
- IRS gave incorrect advice
- Other circumstances beyond your control

## Offers in Compromise

### What Is an OIC?

An agreement with the IRS to settle your tax liability for less than the full amount owed.

### Types of OIC

1. **Doubt as to Liability:** You disagree that you owe the tax
2. **Doubt as to Collectibility:** You can't pay full amount
3. **Effective Tax Administration:** You can pay, but it would create economic hardship

### Requirements

- Filed all required returns
- Made all required estimated payments (current year)
- Not in bankruptcy
- Valid extension if filing during extension period

### Application Fee

$205 (waived for low-income taxpayers)

### Form 656

Use **Form 656** and **Form 433-A (OIC)** to apply.

### Offer Amount

Generally based on:
- Reasonable collection potential (RCP)
- Your assets and income
- Allowable living expenses

---

## Currently Not Collectible (CNC)

### What Is CNC Status?

If you can't pay any amount, the IRS may temporarily delay collection.

### Effects

- Collection actions temporarily stop
- Interest and penalties continue to accrue
- Tax debt doesn't go away
- IRS will review periodically

### How to Request

Contact the IRS and provide financial information demonstrating inability to pay.

---

## Refund Statute of Limitations

### Claiming a Refund

You generally have **3 years** from the date you filed the return (or 2 years from the date you paid the tax, whichever is later) to claim a refund.

### If Filed Late

If you didn't file by the due date, the 3-year period starts from the date you actually filed.

### If You Don't File

Unclaimed refunds expire after 3 years from the original due date.

---

## Summary: What to Do

| Situation | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| Expecting refund | Choose direct deposit for fastest refund |
| Owe tax, can pay | Pay by due date to avoid penalties |
| Owe tax, can't pay in full | File return, pay what you can, request payment plan |
| Owe large amount | Consider installment agreement |
| Can't pay anything | Contact IRS about Currently Not Collectible status |
| Doubt you owe the full amount | Consider Offer in Compromise |
