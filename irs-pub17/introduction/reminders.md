## Identity Protection

### Secure Your Tax Records from Identity Theft
Identity theft occurs when someone uses your personal information, such as your name, SSN, or other identifying information, without your permission, to commit fraud or other crimes.

**How identity thieves may use your SSN:**
- Get a job
- File a tax return to receive a refund

For more information about identity theft and how to reduce your risk, see [Chapter 1: Filing Information](../part1/chapter-01-filing-information.md).

### Identity Verification
The IRS uses **ID.me** as a trusted technology provider for identity verification and sign-in. This mobile-friendly verification procedure provides access to:
- Child Tax Credit Update Portal
- Online Account
- Get Transcript Online
- Get an Identity Protection PIN (IP PIN)
- Online Payment Agreement

See IR-2021-228 for more information.

## Taxpayer Identification Numbers (TINs)

You must provide the TIN for each person for whom you claim certain tax benefits. This applies even if the person was born in 2025.

**Generally, the TIN is the person's Social Security Number (SSN).**

See [Chapter 1](../part1/chapter-01-filing-information.md).

## Credits

### Additional Child Tax Credit (ACTC)
- **Maximum amount:** $1,700 per qualifying child
- **Puerto Rico residents:** No longer required to have three or more qualifying children to claim ACTC. May be eligible with one or more qualifying children.

### Premium Tax Credit (PTC)
The ARP expanded the PTC by:
- Eliminating the limitation that household income may not exceed 400% of the federal poverty line
- Generally increasing credit amounts

See Pub. 974 and Form 8962 instructions.

## Foreign Income and Assets

### Foreign-Source Income
If you are a U.S. citizen with income from sources outside the United States (foreign income), you must report **all such income** on your tax return unless exempt by law or tax treaty.

**This is true whether:**
- You live inside or outside the United States
- You receive a Form W-2 or Form 1099 from the foreign payer

**Applies to:**
- Earned income (wages, tips)
- Unearned income (interest, dividends, capital gains, pensions, rents, royalties)

If you live outside the United States, you may be able to exclude part or all of your foreign earned income. See Pub. 54.

### Foreign Financial Assets
If you had foreign financial assets in 2025, you may have to file **Form 8938** with your return.

See [IRS.gov/Form8938](https://www.irs.gov/form8938).
