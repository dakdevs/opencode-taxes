## Introduction

A **dependent** is either a qualifying child or a qualifying relative. You may be able to claim benefits related to having a dependent, including:
- Dependency exemption (currently $0, but may affect other benefits)
- Child Tax Credit
- Credit for Other Dependents
- Earned Income Credit
- Child and Dependent Care Credit
- Head of Household filing status

---

## Who Can Be Claimed as a Dependent

### General Rules for All Dependents

To claim someone as a dependent, ALL of the following must be true:

1. **Dependent Taxpayer Test:** The person cannot be claimed as a dependent on anyone else's return.

2. **Joint Return Test:** The person cannot file a joint return with their spouse (exception: filing only to claim a refund and no tax liability would exist for either spouse).

3. **Citizen or Resident Test:** The person must be:
   - A U.S. citizen
   - A U.S. resident alien
   - A U.S. national, or
   - A resident of Canada or Mexico

Then, the person must meet the tests for either a **Qualifying Child** or a **Qualifying Relative**.

---

## Qualifying Child

A qualifying child must meet ALL of the following tests:

### 1. Relationship Test

The child must be your:
- Son, daughter, stepchild, foster child, or a descendant of any of them (grandchild, great-grandchild)
- Brother, sister, half-brother, half-sister, stepbrother, stepsister
- A descendant of your sibling (niece, nephew)

> **Note:** An adopted child is treated the same as a biological child.

### 2. Age Test

The child must be:
- Under age 19 at the end of the year, **or**
- Under age 24 at the end of the year AND a full-time student, **or**
- Any age if permanently and totally disabled

> **Full-time student:** Enrolled full-time for at least 5 months during the year at a school with regular teaching staff, courses, and enrolled student body.

### 3. Residency Test

The child must have lived with you for **more than half the year**.

**Exceptions for temporary absences:**
- School
- Vacation
- Business
- Medical care
- Military service
- Detention in a juvenile facility

**Special rules apply for:**
- Children of divorced or separated parents
- Kidnapped children
- Children who were born or died during the year

### 4. Support Test

The child must **not** have provided more than half of their own support for the year.

### 5. Joint Return Test

The child can't file a joint return for the year (unless filing only to claim a refund).

---

## Qualifying Relative

If a person isn't your qualifying child, they may still be your qualifying relative. They must meet ALL of the following tests:

### 1. Not a Qualifying Child Test

The person isn't the qualifying child of you or any other taxpayer.

### 2. Member of Household or Relationship Test

The person must either:
- Live with you all year as a member of your household, **or**
- Be related to you in one of these ways (doesn't need to live with you):
  - Child, stepchild, foster child, or descendant
  - Brother, sister, half-brother, half-sister, stepbrother, stepsister
  - Parent, stepparent, or ancestor (grandparent, great-grandparent)
  - Niece, nephew
  - Aunt, uncle
  - Son-in-law, daughter-in-law, father-in-law, mother-in-law, brother-in-law, sister-in-law

> **Note:** Relationships established by marriage aren't ended by death or divorce.

### 3. Gross Income Test

The person's gross income for the year must be less than **$5,200** (for 2025).

**Gross income includes:**
- Earned income (wages, salary, tips)
- Unearned income (interest, dividends, capital gains)
- Taxable scholarship amounts

**Gross income does NOT include:**
- Tax-exempt income
- Social Security benefits (unless taxable)

### 4. Support Test

You must provide **more than half** of the person's total support for the year.

---

## Support Test Details

### What Counts as Support

| Include | Don't Include |
|---------|---------------|
| Food | Federal, state, local income taxes paid by person |
| Lodging (fair rental value) | Social Security and Medicare taxes paid by person |
| Clothing | Life insurance premiums |
| Education | Funeral expenses |
| Medical and dental care | Scholarships received |
| Recreation | |
| Transportation | |
| Similar necessities | |

### Fair Rental Value

If the person lives with you, figure the fair rental value of the lodging you provide. This is the amount you could reasonably expect to receive from a stranger for the same kind of lodging.

### Multiple Support Agreement

If no one person provides more than half of a person's support, but two or more people together provide more than half, one person may claim the dependent if:
- You paid more than 10% of the person's support
- You could have claimed the person except for the support test
- Each other person who paid more than 10% signs a **Form 2120** agreeing not to claim the person

## Special Rules

### Children of Divorced or Separated Parents

The **custodial parent** (the parent with whom the child lived for the greater part of the year) is generally entitled to claim the child as a dependent.

**Exception:** The noncustodial parent can claim the child if:
- The custodial parent signs **Form 8332** releasing the claim to the exemption, **or**
- A pre-1985 divorce decree gives the exemption to the noncustodial parent

> **Note:** Even if the noncustodial parent claims the child, the custodial parent may still be able to claim:
> - Head of Household status
> - Earned Income Credit
> - Child and Dependent Care Credit

### Child Who Qualifies for More Than One Person

If a child meets the tests to be a qualifying child of more than one person, only ONE person can claim the child. Use these tie-breaker rules:

1. **Parent vs. Non-Parent:** If only one is a parent, the parent claims the child.

2. **Both Parents (Don't File Jointly):** The parent with whom the child lived longer claims the child. If the child lived with each parent the same amount of time, the parent with the higher AGI claims the child.

3. **Neither Is a Parent:** The person with the highest AGI claims the child.

### Kidnapped Child

You may be able to claim a child as a dependent if the child was kidnapped, if certain conditions are met.

## Social Security Numbers for Dependents

### Requirement

You must provide an SSN for each dependent you claim on your return.

### Getting an SSN for a Child

Apply using **Form SS-5** at the Social Security Administration.

### Child Born and Died in 2025

If your child was born and died in 2025 and didn't have an SSN, you can:
- Enter "DIED" in the space for the SSN, **and**
- Attach a copy of the child's birth certificate, death certificate, or hospital records

### ITIN for Non-Citizen Dependents

If your dependent isn't eligible for an SSN, you may need to obtain an **Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN)** using Form W-7.

## Summary Table: Qualifying Child vs. Qualifying Relative

| Test | Qualifying Child | Qualifying Relative |
|------|-----------------|---------------------|
| **Relationship** | Child, sibling, or descendant | Live-in or related person |
| **Age** | Under 19, or under 24 if student, or any age if disabled | Any age |
| **Residency** | More than half the year | Entire year (unless related) |
| **Support** | Child can't provide more than half own support | YOU must provide more than half |
| **Gross Income** | No limit | Less than $5,200 |

---

## Tax Benefits for Dependents

| Benefit | Qualifying Child | Qualifying Relative |
|---------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Child Tax Credit (up to $2,000) | Yes* | No |
| Credit for Other Dependents ($500) | No** | Yes |
| Earned Income Credit | Yes | No |
| Child and Dependent Care Credit | Yes | Yes (if certain conditions met) |
| Head of Household | Yes | Yes (parent only) |

*Child must be under 17 and meet other requirements
**Qualifying children who don't qualify for child tax credit may qualify for credit for other dependents
