## Introduction

Interest income is generally taxable and must be reported on your tax return. However, some interest is tax-exempt.

**Form 1099-INT:** Most payers of interest will send you a Form 1099-INT if you earned $10 or more in interest during the year. Even if you don't receive a Form 1099-INT, you must still report all taxable interest.

---

## Taxable Interest

### Common Sources of Taxable Interest

| Source | Taxable |
|--------|---------|
| Bank accounts (savings, checking, CD) | Yes |
| Credit union accounts | Yes |
| Money market accounts | Yes |
| Corporate bonds | Yes |
| U.S. Treasury bills, notes, bonds | Yes (federal); No (state/local) |
| U.S. Savings Bonds (Series EE, I) | Yes (can defer) |
| Mortgage-backed securities | Yes |
| Original issue discount (OID) | Yes |
| Interest on tax refunds | Yes |
| Seller-financed mortgages | Yes |
| Insurance dividends (if exceed premiums paid) | Yes |

### Interest You Must Report

Even if you don't receive Form 1099-INT, you must report:
- Interest credited to your account even if not withdrawn
- Interest received on insurance dividends left on deposit
- Interest on frozen deposits (when withdrawn or available)
- Interest received as a beneficiary
- Imputed interest on below-market loans

### Nominee Interest

If you receive Form 1099-INT that includes interest belonging to someone else:
1. Report the full amount on your return
2. Deduct the nominee portion on Schedule B
3. File Form 1099-INT for the actual owner if $10 or more

## Tax-Exempt Interest

### State and Local Bonds

Interest on bonds issued by state and local governments is generally **exempt from federal income tax**.

> **Note:** While tax-exempt, you must still report this interest on your return. Enter it on Form 1040, line 2a.

### Private Activity Bonds

Interest on certain private activity bonds may be:
- Tax-exempt for regular tax purposes, **but**
- Subject to Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT)

### U.S. Savings Bond Interest Exclusion

Interest on Series EE and I bonds may be tax-free if:
- You pay qualified higher education expenses
- Bonds were issued after 1989
- You were at least 24 years old when bonds were issued
- Income is below phase-out limits

**2025 Phase-Out (Education Bond Exclusion):**
| Filing Status | Phase-Out Begins | Complete Phase-Out |
|--------------|------------------|-------------------|
| MFJ | $145,200 | $175,200 |
| Single/HOH | $96,800 | $111,800 |

Use **Form 8815** to claim this exclusion.

## When To Report Interest

### Cash Method Taxpayers

Most individuals use the cash method. Report interest in the year you:
- Actually receive it, **or**
- Constructively receive it (credited to your account)

### Constructive Receipt

Interest is constructively received when it's credited to your account or made available to you, even if you don't withdraw it.

**Examples:**
- Interest credited to savings account quarterly
- CD interest credited at maturity
- Bond coupon interest on payment date

### Interest on Frozen Deposits

If you can't withdraw interest because of bankruptcy or financial condition of the institution, don't report it until you can withdraw it.

## Original Issue Discount (OID)

### What Is OID?

OID is a form of interest. It's the difference between:
- The stated redemption price at maturity, **and**
- The issue price

### When OID Applies

OID applies to:
- Zero-coupon bonds
- Bonds issued at a discount
- Certain CDs and time deposits

### Reporting OID

You must include OID in income as it accrues over the life of the obligation, even if you don't receive any payments.

**Form 1099-OID:** You should receive this form showing the OID to report.

### De Minimis OID

If the discount is less than 1/4 of 1% (0.25%) of the stated redemption price at maturity multiplied by the number of years to maturity, you can:
- Treat it as zero, **or**
- Include it in income when the bond matures or you sell it

## U.S. Savings Bonds

### Series EE and Series I Bonds

You can report interest in one of two ways:

**Method 1: Report interest each year as it accrues**
- Report even though you don't receive actual interest payments
- Once chosen, must continue for all Series EE/I bonds

**Method 2: Defer reporting until the bonds are redeemed**
- Most taxpayers use this method
- Report all accumulated interest when you cash the bond

### Changing Methods

To change from Method 2 to Method 1:
- Report all interest accrued to date that you haven't previously reported
- No IRS permission needed
- Must continue to report annually for all bonds

### Transferred Bonds

If you transfer Series EE or I bonds:

| Type of Transfer | Who Reports Interest |
|------------------|---------------------|
| Redemption (you cash) | You report all interest |
| Gift | You report accrued interest through transfer date; recipient reports rest |
| Inheritance | Decedent's final return OR estate/beneficiary |
| Co-owner to co-owner | Original co-owner reports interest |

### Series HH Bonds

- Interest is paid semiannually
- Report in the year received
- Series HH bonds are no longer issued

## How To Report

### Form 1040 Reporting

**Interest income goes on:**
- **Line 2a:** Tax-exempt interest
- **Line 2b:** Taxable interest

### Schedule B Required If:

You must file Schedule B if:
- Your taxable interest exceeds **$1,500**
- You received interest as a nominee
- You received interest from a seller-financed mortgage
- You have accrued interest from a bond
- You're claiming the education savings bond exclusion
- You have a financial interest in, or signature authority over, a foreign financial account

### Example: Schedule B

```
Part I - Interest
                                        Amount
1. ABC Bank                            $  450.00
   XYZ Credit Union                       325.00
   U.S. Treasury Bills                    890.00
   Corporate Bond Fund                    235.00
2. Add the amounts                     $1,900.00
3. Excludable interest on Series EE bonds    0
4. Total (enter on Form 1040, line 2b) $1,900.00
```

## Special Situations

### Interest on Insurance

**Life insurance dividends:** Not taxable unless they exceed premiums paid.

**Dividends left on deposit:** Interest earned on dividends left with insurance company is taxable.

### Below-Market Loans

If you make a loan with below-market interest:
- Lender may have to report imputed interest as income
- Borrower may be able to deduct imputed interest (if otherwise deductible)

**Exceptions exist for:**
- Loans of $10,000 or less (non-tax avoidance)
- Loans of $100,000 or less (limited to borrower's net investment income)

### Interest on Seller-Financed Sales

If you sell property and finance it for the buyer:
- Report the interest portion of payments as interest income
- Must provide buyer with your name, address, and TIN
