## Introduction

If you itemize deductions on Schedule A, you can deduct certain taxes you paid during the year.

> **Important 2025 Change:** The SALT deduction limit has increased to **$40,000** ($20,000 if married filing separately).

---

## State and Local Taxes (SALT)

### What You Can Deduct

You can deduct either:
- **State and local income taxes**, **OR**
- **State and local general sales taxes**

You cannot deduct both. Choose whichever gives you the larger deduction.

### 2025 SALT Deduction Limit

| Filing Status | Maximum SALT Deduction |
|--------------|----------------------|
| Single | $40,000 |
| Married Filing Jointly | $40,000 |
| Married Filing Separately | $20,000 |
| Head of Household | $40,000 |
| Qualifying Surviving Spouse | $40,000 |

**Phase-Out (New for 2025):**
- The limit is reduced if MAGI exceeds $500,000 ($250,000 MFS)
- Minimum limit: $10,000 ($5,000 MFS)

### State and Local Income Taxes

**Deductible:**
- State income taxes withheld from wages (Form W-2, Box 17)
- Local income taxes withheld
- Estimated state income tax payments made during the year
- State income tax paid in current year for a prior year
- Mandatory contributions to state disability funds
- **New:** Contributions to governmental paid family leave programs

**Not Deductible:**
- Federal income taxes
- State income tax refunds (but may be taxable as income)

### State and Local Sales Taxes

If you elect to deduct sales tax instead of income tax:

**Option 1:** Use IRS tables
- Based on income and family size
- Add sales tax on specific large purchases (vehicles, boats, building materials)

**Option 2:** Actual expenses
- Keep all receipts
- More work, but may result in larger deduction

**IRS Sales Tax Calculator:** [IRS.gov/SalesTax](https://www.irs.gov/salestax)

## Real Estate Taxes

### Deductible Real Estate Taxes

You can deduct real estate taxes imposed on property you own if the taxes are:
- Based on the assessed value of the property
- Charged uniformly against all property in the jurisdiction
- Used for general public or governmental purposes

### Includes

- State and local real property taxes
- Foreign real property taxes (see Foreign Taxes section)

### Does NOT Include

- Taxes for local benefits (sidewalks, streets, sewers) that increase property value
- Homeowners' association fees
- Transfer taxes
- Rent (even if part goes to property taxes)
- Assessments for specific local improvements

### Buying or Selling Property

When property is bought or sold during the year, the taxes are allocated between buyer and seller based on the number of days each owned the property.

**Example:**
Property sold on September 1 (buyer owns 122 days, seller owns 243 days).
Annual tax: $6,000

- Seller's deduction: $6,000 × (243/365) = $3,995
- Buyer's deduction: $6,000 × (122/365) = $2,005

### Escrow Accounts

You can only deduct real estate taxes in the year they're **actually paid** to the taxing authority, not when payments are made to your escrow account.

## Personal Property Taxes

### What's Deductible

You can deduct state and local personal property taxes if they are:
- Based on the value of the property
- Charged on personal property (not real estate)
- Charged annually

### Common Examples

- Annual car registration fees (only the value-based portion)
- Boat or aircraft taxes
- Personal property taxes on RVs

### Vehicle Registration Fees

Many states charge both:
- A flat fee (not deductible)
- A value-based tax (deductible)

Only the **value-based portion** is deductible as a tax.

**Example:**
Annual vehicle registration: $150
- Flat fee portion: $50 (not deductible)
- Value-based portion: $100 (deductible)

## Foreign Taxes

### Foreign Real Property Taxes

You can include foreign real property taxes in your itemized deductions on Schedule A.

### Foreign Income Taxes

You have a choice for foreign income taxes:
- **Deduct** them on Schedule A, **or**
- **Claim a credit** on Form 1116

Generally, taking the credit is more beneficial because it directly reduces your tax (dollar-for-dollar), while a deduction only reduces taxable income.

> **Note:** You cannot both deduct and claim a credit for the same foreign taxes.

### When to Deduct vs. Credit

| Situation | Better Choice |
|-----------|---------------|
| Small amount of foreign tax | Credit (may not need Form 1116 if under $300/$600) |
| Foreign tax exceeds U.S. tax on foreign income | May need to deduct |
| You don't itemize | Can still claim credit |
| Complex foreign tax situations | Consult tax professional |

## Taxes You Cannot Deduct

### Federal Taxes

- Federal income taxes
- Social Security taxes (FICA)
- Medicare taxes
- Federal unemployment taxes
- Federal excise taxes
- Estate or gift taxes

### State and Local Taxes Not Deductible

- State or local taxes not based on income or value
- Fines and penalties
- Dog licenses
- Hunting and fishing licenses
- Fees for services (trash collection, water)
- Parking fees and tickets
- Driver's license fees
- Tolls

### Other Non-Deductible Items

- Taxes on property you don't own
- Taxes paid for another person (unless for a dependent)
- Home mortgage insurance premiums
- Homeowners' association fees

### Worksheet

```

3. Estimated state/local tax payments           $_______
4. Prior year state tax paid this year          $_______
5. Paid family leave contributions              $_______
6. Subtotal (add lines 1-5)                     $_______

OR

Sales tax from IRS tables or actual             $_______
Plus sales tax on major purchases               $_______
7. Total sales tax                              $_______

8. GREATER of line 6 or line 7                  $_______ (A)

REAL ESTATE TAXES:
9. Real estate taxes paid                       $_______ (B)

PERSONAL PROPERTY TAXES:
10. Value-based vehicle registration            $_______
11. Other personal property taxes               $_______
12. Total personal property taxes               $_______ (C)

TOTAL SALT:
13. Add lines A + B + C                         $_______

14. SALT LIMIT                                  $40,000
    (or $20,000 if MFS)

15. DEDUCTIBLE AMOUNT
    (lesser of line 13 or line 14)              $_______
```

## State Tax Refunds

### Are They Taxable?

If you received a state or local income tax refund and you itemized deductions the previous year:
- The refund may be **taxable income** in the year received
- Only taxable to the extent you received a **tax benefit**

### Tax Benefit Rule

You only include the refund in income if, and to the extent that, your state tax deduction reduced your federal tax in the prior year.

**Example:**
2024: You deducted $45,000 in SALT but were limited to $40,000.
2025: You receive a $3,000 state tax refund.

The refund is tax-free because you didn't get a tax benefit from the $5,000 ($45,000 - $40,000) that exceeded the limit.

### Reporting

Report taxable refunds on Schedule 1, line 1.

---

## Special Situations

### Refundable State Tax Credits

If you receive a refundable state tax credit, it reduces your SALT deduction in the year received (not a taxable refund).

### Property Tax Deduction for Homeowners

You can only deduct property taxes on your:
- Main home
- Second home

Property taxes on rental property are deducted on Schedule E, not Schedule A.

### Governmental Paid Family Leave Programs (New)

Beginning in 2025:
- Include the full amount of contributions in income
- If you itemize, deduct as part of state/local income taxes paid
