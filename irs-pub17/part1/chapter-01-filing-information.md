## Do You Have To File?

### Filing Requirements

Whether you must file a federal income tax return depends on your:
- Gross income
- Filing status
- Age
- Whether you are a dependent

### 2025 Filing Requirements by Filing Status

You must file a return if your gross income was at least:

| Filing Status | Age | Gross Income Threshold |
|--------------|-----|----------------------|
| Single | Under 65 | $15,750 |
| Single | 65 or older | $18,300 |
| Head of household | Under 65 | $23,625 |
| Head of household | 65 or older | $26,175 |
| Married filing jointly | Both under 65 | $31,500 |
| Married filing jointly | One spouse 65+ | $33,450 |
| Married filing jointly | Both 65+ | $35,400 |
| Married filing separately | Any age | $5 |
| Qualifying surviving spouse | Under 65 | $31,500 |
| Qualifying surviving spouse | 65 or older | $33,450 |

> **Note:** These amounts are equal to the standard deduction for your filing status, plus any additional standard deduction for age 65 or older.

### Gross Income Defined

**Gross income** means all income you received in the form of money, goods, property, and services that isn't exempt from tax, including any income from sources outside the United States or from the sale of your main home (even if you can exclude part or all of it).

**Gross income includes, but is not limited to:**
- Wages, salaries, and tips
- Interest and dividends
- Business income
- Capital gains
- IRA distributions
- Pensions and annuities
- Rental income
- Farm income
- Unemployment compensation
- Social Security benefits (if taxable)
- Alimony received (for divorce agreements executed before 2019)

### Special Filing Requirements

You must file a return if any of the following apply:

1. **Self-Employment Income:** You had net self-employment earnings of at least $400.

2. **Household Employee Wages:** You received wages of $2,700 or more from any one household employer.

3. **Advance Premium Tax Credit:** You, your spouse, or a dependent enrolled in health insurance through the Marketplace and advance payments of the premium tax credit were made.

4. **Health Coverage Exemption:** You need to claim an exemption from the shared responsibility payment.

5. **Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT):** You owe AMT.

6. **Additional Tax on Qualified Plans:** You owe additional tax on a qualified plan, including an IRA.

7. **Social Security/Medicare Tax on Tips:** You owe Social Security and Medicare tax on tips you didn't report to your employer.

8. **Write-in Taxes:** You owe write-in taxes, including uncollected Social Security and Medicare tax on tips or group-term life insurance.

9. **Recapture of Credits:** You must recapture education credits, investment credit, or other credits.

## Who Should File

Even if you don't have to file, you should consider filing if:

### To Get a Refund
You should file to get a refund if:
- You had federal income tax withheld from your pay
- You made estimated tax payments
- You qualify for refundable credits such as:
  - Earned Income Credit (EIC)
  - Additional Child Tax Credit (ACTC)
  - American Opportunity Credit
  - Premium Tax Credit

### To Protect Your Refund
Generally, you must file a return within **3 years** from the date the return was due (including extensions) to get a refund.

### For Other Benefits
You may want to file to:
- Establish a record of your income with Social Security
- Obtain a record of filing for loan applications
- Request an extension if you need more time

## How and When To File

### When To File

**Regular Deadline:** April 15, 2026

If you file on a calendar year basis, your 2025 return is due **April 15, 2026**.

> **Note:** If the due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the due date is moved to the next business day.

### Extensions

If you can't file by the due date:

1. **Automatic Extension:** File **Form 4868** by April 15, 2026, for an automatic 6-month extension (until October 15, 2026).

2. **Payment Still Due:** An extension of time to file is NOT an extension of time to pay. You must estimate and pay any tax due by the original due date.

3. **Out of the Country:** If you live and work outside the United States and Puerto Rico, you get an automatic 2-month extension (until June 16, 2026) without filing Form 4868.

4. **Combat Zone:** Special extensions apply if you serve in a combat zone.

### Where To File

**Electronic Filing (E-file):** The IRS recommends e-filing. You can:
- Use IRS Free File if your AGI is $84,000 or less
- Use tax preparation software
- Use a tax professional

**Paper Filing:** If you file a paper return, mail it to the address shown in the Form 1040 instructions for your state.

See [Where To File](../appendix/where-to-file.md) for specific addresses.

### Amended Returns

If you need to change a return you already filed, file **Form 1040-X**.

- You can now e-file Form 1040-X
- Generally, you must file within 3 years of the original due date
- If you're filing to claim a refund, you must file within 3 years of the original due date or 2 years of when you paid the tax, whichever is later

## What Form To Use

### Form 1040 and Form 1040-SR

Most individual taxpayers use:
- **Form 1040** - U.S. Individual Income Tax Return
- **Form 1040-SR** - U.S. Tax Return for Seniors (for taxpayers age 65 and older)

Both forms can be used regardless of filing status.

### Form 1040-SR Features

Form 1040-SR is designed for seniors with:
- Larger print
- Standard deduction chart included on the form
- Same schedules and attachments as Form 1040

### Common Schedules

| Schedule | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| Schedule 1 | Additional Income and Adjustments to Income |
| Schedule 1-A | New Deductions (tips, overtime, car loan interest, senior deduction) |
| Schedule 2 | Additional Taxes |
| Schedule 3 | Additional Credits and Payments |
| Schedule A | Itemized Deductions |
| Schedule B | Interest and Ordinary Dividends |
| Schedule C | Profit or Loss From Business |
| Schedule D | Capital Gains and Losses |
| Schedule E | Supplemental Income and Loss |
| Schedule SE | Self-Employment Tax |

## Identity Protection

### Protecting Your Identity

Identity theft occurs when someone uses your personal information (such as your name, SSN, or other identifying information) without your permission to commit fraud.

**Tax-related identity theft** happens when someone uses your SSN to:
- File a fraudulent tax return and claim a refund
- Get a job

### Warning Signs of Tax-Related Identity Theft

- You receive a letter from the IRS about a return you didn't file
- You can't e-file because a return was already filed using your SSN
- You receive a tax transcript you didn't request
- You receive an IRS notice about income from an employer you don't know

### What To Do If You're a Victim

1. **Respond immediately** to any IRS notice
2. **Complete Form 14039**, Identity Theft Affidavit
3. **Continue to file** your tax return, even if you must paper-file
4. **Monitor** your credit reports

### Identity Protection PIN (IP PIN)

An IP PIN is a 6-digit number that helps prevent someone from filing a fraudulent return using your SSN.

**To get an IP PIN:**
- Go to [IRS.gov/IPPIN](https://www.irs.gov/ippin)
- Use the Get an IP PIN tool
- Once enrolled, you'll receive a new IP PIN each year

## Social Security Number (SSN)

### Requirement

You must enter your SSN on your return. If you file a joint return, enter both your SSN and your spouse's SSN.

### Getting an SSN

If you don't have an SSN, apply for one using **Form SS-5** from the Social Security Administration.

### Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN)

If you're not eligible for an SSN but need a tax identification number, apply for an ITIN using **Form W-7**.

### SSN for Dependents

You must provide an SSN for each dependent you claim. If your dependent was born and died in 2025 and didn't have an SSN, enter "DIED" in the space for the SSN.

## What Happens After You File

### Processing Your Return

- **E-filed returns:** Usually processed within 21 days
- **Paper returns:** Usually processed within 6-8 weeks

### Checking Your Refund Status

**Online:** [IRS.gov/Refunds](https://www.irs.gov/refunds) or the IRS2Go mobile app

**By Phone:** 1-800-829-1954

**Information needed:**
- Your SSN
- Your filing status
- Your exact refund amount

### If You Owe Tax

**Payment Options:**
- Direct Pay from your bank account
- Electronic Federal Tax Payment System (EFTPS)
- Credit or debit card
- Check or money order

If you can't pay in full, consider:
- **Installment agreement:** Apply online or use Form 9465
- **Offer in compromise:** If you can't pay your full tax liability

### IRS Notices

If the IRS needs more information or finds an error, you'll receive a notice in the mail. **Always respond promptly** to IRS notices.
