# 2025 Retirement Contribution Limits

## IRA Contribution Limits

### Traditional and Roth IRA

| Age | Contribution Limit |
|-----|-------------------|
| Under 50 | $7,000 |
| 50 or older | $8,000 (includes $1,000 catch-up) |

**Note**: This is the combined limit for Traditional and Roth IRAs.

---

## Roth IRA Income Phase-Outs

Ability to contribute to Roth IRA phases out at higher incomes.

### Single, Head of Household

| MAGI | Contribution Allowed |
|------|---------------------|
| $150,000 or less | Full contribution |
| $150,001 - $165,000 | Partial contribution |
| Over $165,000 | No contribution |

### Married Filing Jointly

| MAGI | Contribution Allowed |
|------|---------------------|
| $236,000 or less | Full contribution |
| $236,001 - $246,000 | Partial contribution |
| Over $246,000 | No contribution |

### Married Filing Separately

| MAGI | Contribution Allowed |
|------|---------------------|
| $0 - $10,000 | Partial contribution |
| Over $10,000 | No contribution |

### Partial Contribution Calculation

```
1. MAGI:                                          $_______
2. Phase-out floor:                               $_______
3. Excess (Line 1 - Line 2):                      $_______
4. Phase-out range (from table):                  $_______
5. Phase-out percentage (Line 3 ÷ Line 4):        _______%
6. Maximum contribution:                          $_______
7. Reduction (Line 6 × Line 5):                   $_______
8. Allowed contribution (Line 6 - Line 7):        $_______
   (Round up to nearest $10, minimum $200)
```

## Traditional IRA Deduction Phase-Outs

If you're covered by a workplace retirement plan, deduction phases out.

### Single, Head of Household (Covered by Plan)

| MAGI | Deduction Allowed |
|------|------------------|
| $79,000 or less | Full deduction |
| $79,001 - $89,000 | Partial deduction |
| Over $89,000 | No deduction |

### Married Filing Jointly (Both Covered)

| MAGI | Deduction Allowed |
|------|------------------|
| $126,000 or less | Full deduction |
| $126,001 - $146,000 | Partial deduction |
| Over $146,000 | No deduction |

### MFJ (One Spouse Covered)

| MAGI | Deduction for Non-Covered Spouse |
|------|----------------------------------|
| $236,000 or less | Full deduction |
| $236,001 - $246,000 | Partial deduction |
| Over $246,000 | No deduction |

**Not Covered by Workplace Plan**: Full deduction regardless of income.

## 401(k), 403(b), 457 Plans

### Employee Contribution Limits

| Age | Contribution Limit |
|-----|-------------------|
| Under 50 | $23,500 |
| 50-59 or 64+ | $31,000 (includes $7,500 catch-up) |
| 60-63 | $34,750 (includes $11,250 super catch-up) |

### Total Contribution Limit (Employee + Employer)

| Age | Total Limit |
|-----|-------------|
| Under 50 | $70,000 |
| 50+ | $77,500 |

---

## Solo 401(k) for Self-Employed

### Employee Contribution
- Same as regular 401(k): $23,500 (under 50) / $31,000 (50+)

### Employer Contribution
- Up to 25% of net self-employment income
- Net SE income = Profit - (1/2 SE tax) - Employee contribution

### Total Maximum
- $70,000 (under 50)
- $77,500 (50+)

### Solo 401(k) Calculation

```
1. Net self-employment income:                    $_______
2. SE tax deduction (1/2 of SE tax):              $_______
3. Adjusted SE income (Line 1 - Line 2):          $_______
4. Employer contribution (Line 3 × 25%):          $_______
5. Employee contribution (max $23,500/$31,000):   $_______
6. Total contribution (Line 4 + Line 5):          $_______
   (Cannot exceed $70,000/$77,500)
```

---

## SEP-IRA for Self-Employed

### Contribution Limit
- **25%** of net self-employment income, OR
- **$70,000**, whichever is less

### SEP-IRA Calculation

```
1. Net self-employment income:                    $_______
2. SE tax deduction:                              $_______
3. Adjusted SE income (Line 1 - Line 2):          $_______
4. Contribution rate: 20% (not 25%)*              × 0.20
5. Maximum contribution (Line 3 × 0.20):          $_______
   (Cannot exceed $70,000)

* For self-employed, the effective rate is 20% because the 
  contribution is based on income after the contribution.
```

## Backdoor Roth IRA

If income exceeds Roth IRA limits:

1. Contribute to **non-deductible Traditional IRA**
2. Convert to **Roth IRA**
3. Pay tax on any gains between contribution and conversion

**Warning**: Pro-rata rule applies if you have existing Traditional IRA balances.
