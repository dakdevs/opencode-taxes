## Introduction

This chapter discusses wages, salaries, and other compensation received for services you performed as an employee.

**General Rule:** All compensation for personal services, in whatever form paid, is taxable unless specifically excluded by law.

---

## Compensation for Services

### What's Included in Wages

Your wages include all pay you receive for services performed. This includes:

| Type | Examples |
|------|----------|
| **Cash Payments** | Wages, salaries, commissions, fees |
| **Non-Cash Payments** | Fair market value of goods, stock, property |
| **Incentive Payments** | Bonuses, prizes, awards |
| **Other** | Vacation pay, severance pay, back pay |

### Form W-2

Your employer must give you a **Form W-2** by January 31, showing:
- Your total wages, tips, and other compensation (Box 1)
- Federal income tax withheld (Box 2)
- Social Security wages and tax (Boxes 3, 4)
- Medicare wages and tax (Boxes 5, 6)
- Tips reported (Box 7)
- Allocated tips (Box 8)

> **Note:** Keep your Form W-2s for at least 3 years after filing your return.

### Wage Reporting

Report wages on Form 1040:
- **Line 1a:** Wages from Form W-2, Box 1
- **Schedule 1:** Other income types

## Bonuses and Awards

### Employee Achievement Awards

**Excludable (up to limits):**
- Tangible personal property
- Given for length of service or safety achievement
- Part of a meaningful presentation

**Exclusion Limits:**
| Award Type | Maximum Exclusion |
|------------|------------------|
| Non-qualified plan | $400 per employee per year |
| Qualified plan | $1,600 per employee per year |

**Not Excludable:**
- Cash, cash equivalents, gift cards
- Meals, lodging, tickets
- Securities

### Other Bonuses

All other bonuses are fully taxable, including:
- Holiday bonuses
- Performance bonuses
- Signing bonuses
- Referral bonuses

## Tips

### Taxable Tips

All tips you receive are income and are subject to federal income tax. This includes:
- Cash tips from customers
- Tips from credit card charges
- Tips from a tip-splitting arrangement
- Non-cash tips (fair market value)

### Reporting Tips to Your Employer

If you receive **$20 or more in tips** in any month from one job, you must report them to your employer by the 10th of the following month.

**How to report:**
- Use **Form 4070**, Employee's Report of Tips to Employer, **or**
- Any similar statement

### Tips Not Reported to Employer

If you didn't report tips to your employer, you must report them on your tax return:
- File **Form 4137**, Social Security and Medicare Tax on Unreported Tip Income
- Pay Social Security and Medicare tax on unreported tips

### New for 2025: No Tax on Tips Deduction

You may be eligible to deduct up to **$25,000** of qualified tips.

**Requirements:**
- You have a valid SSN
- If married, you must file jointly
- MAGI limit: $150,000 ($300,000 MFJ)

See [Schedule 1-A](../introduction/whats-new.md#1-no-tax-on-tips) for details.

### Allocated Tips

If you work in a large food or beverage establishment and the tips reported by employees are less than 8% of gross sales, your employer may allocate tips to you.

- Allocated tips appear in **Box 8** of Form W-2
- Allocated tips are **not included** in Box 1
- You may need to report allocated tips as income if your reported tips were less than your share

## Fringe Benefits

### Taxable Fringe Benefits

The value of most fringe benefits is taxable. Common taxable fringe benefits include:

| Benefit | Taxable Amount |
|---------|---------------|
| Personal use of company car | Fair market value minus employee payment |
| Employer-provided cell phone (personal use) | Fair market value |
| Gym membership | Full value |
| Moving expenses (except military) | Full value |
| Educational assistance over $5,250 | Excess amount |

### Tax-Free Fringe Benefits

Certain fringe benefits are excluded from income:

| Benefit | Exclusion Limit |
|---------|----------------|
| **Health insurance premiums** | Unlimited (employer-paid) |
| **Life insurance** | First $50,000 of coverage |
| **Dependent care assistance** | $5,000 per year |
| **Educational assistance** | $5,250 per year |
| **Adoption assistance** | $17,280 per child (2025) |
| **Transit and parking** | $325/month transit; $325/month parking |
| **Meals (certain conditions)** | De minimis or on-premises |
| **Employee discounts** | Up to gross profit percentage |

### Working Condition Benefits

You can exclude benefits that, if you paid for them yourself, would be deductible as employee business expenses. Examples:
- Professional dues
- Business-related magazines
- Work-related education

### De Minimis Benefits

Small benefits that are administratively impractical to account for are excluded:
- Coffee, snacks
- Occasional personal use of copier
- Holiday gifts of low value
- Occasional entertainment tickets

## Special Rules

### Overtime Pay

Overtime pay is fully taxable as wages.

**New for 2025:** You may be eligible to deduct up to **$12,500** ($25,000 MFJ) of qualified overtime compensation.

**Requirements:**
- Valid SSN
- If married, must file jointly
- MAGI limit: $150,000 ($300,000 MFJ)

See [Schedule 1-A](../introduction/whats-new.md#2-no-tax-on-overtime) for details.

### Sick Pay

Sick pay is generally taxable as wages if paid by your employer or their agent.

**Third-party sick pay:** If paid by an insurance company, it's taxable unless you paid the premiums with after-tax dollars.

### Disability Payments

| Source | Tax Treatment |
|--------|---------------|
| Employer-funded disability | Taxable |
| Employee-funded disability (after-tax) | Not taxable |
| Social Security disability | May be taxable (see Chapter 8) |
| Workers' compensation | Not taxable |

### Severance Pay

Severance pay is taxable as wages in the year received.

### Back Pay

Back pay is taxable in the year received, but you may be able to use a special method to calculate tax if it's for a prior year.

### Stock Compensation

| Type | When Taxed |
|------|-----------|
| **Restricted stock** | When restrictions lapse (or when granted if 83(b) election made) |
| **Stock options (ISO)** | When stock is sold (may trigger AMT at exercise) |
| **Stock options (NQSO)** | When exercised (difference between FMV and exercise price) |
| **ESPP** | When stock is sold |

### Minister's Housing Allowance

Ministers may exclude from income:
- The fair rental value of a home provided by the church, **or**
- A housing allowance designated by the church (limited to the lesser of actual expenses or fair rental value)

## Form W-2 Box Reference

| Box | Description |
|-----|-------------|
| 1 | Wages, tips, other compensation (report on Form 1040, line 1a) |
| 2 | Federal income tax withheld |
| 3 | Social Security wages |
| 4 | Social Security tax withheld |
| 5 | Medicare wages and tips |
| 6 | Medicare tax withheld |
| 7 | Social Security tips |
| 8 | Allocated tips (may need to report separately) |
| 10 | Dependent care benefits |
| 12 | Various codes (retirement contributions, etc.) |
