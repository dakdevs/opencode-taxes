## Introduction

An Individual Retirement Arrangement (IRA) is a personal savings plan that gives you tax advantages for setting aside money for retirement.

### Types of IRAs

| Type | Contributions | Distributions |
|------|---------------|---------------|
| **Traditional IRA** | May be deductible | Taxable |
| **Roth IRA** | Not deductible | Tax-free (if qualified) |
| **SEP IRA** | Employer-funded | Taxable |
| **SIMPLE IRA** | Employee & employer | Taxable |
| **Trump Account** | Up to $5,000/year + $1,000 pilot | Special rules apply |

## Traditional IRAs

### Contribution Limits (2025)

| Age | Contribution Limit |
|-----|-------------------|
| Under 50 | $7,000 |
| 50 or older | $8,000 (includes $1,000 catch-up) |

### Who Can Contribute?

You can contribute to a traditional IRA if:
- You (or your spouse if filing jointly) have taxable compensation
- You weren't age 70½ by the end of 2019 (no age limit starting 2020)

### Contribution Deadline

You have until the **tax filing deadline** (April 15, 2026) to make contributions for 2025.

### Deductibility of Contributions

**If you're NOT covered by a retirement plan at work:**
Your contribution is fully deductible regardless of income.

**If you ARE covered by a retirement plan at work (2025):**

| Filing Status | Full Deduction | Partial Deduction | No Deduction |
|--------------|----------------|-------------------|--------------|
| Single/HOH | MAGI ≤ $79,000 | $79,000-$89,000 | MAGI ≥ $89,000 |
| MFJ | MAGI ≤ $126,000 | $126,000-$146,000 | MAGI ≥ $146,000 |
| MFS | N/A | $0-$10,000 | MAGI ≥ $10,000 |

**If your spouse is covered but you are NOT (2025):**

| Your MAGI | Deduction |
|-----------|-----------|
| ≤ $236,000 | Full deduction |
| $236,000-$246,000 | Partial deduction |
| ≥ $246,000 | No deduction |

### Nondeductible Contributions

If you can't deduct your contribution, you can still make **nondeductible contributions**:
- Report on **Form 8606**
- Track your basis in the IRA
- Only the earnings portion is taxable when distributed

## Roth IRAs

### Key Differences from Traditional IRA

| Feature | Traditional IRA | Roth IRA |
|---------|----------------|----------|
| Contributions | May be deductible | Never deductible |
| Earnings growth | Tax-deferred | Tax-free |
| Qualified distributions | Taxable | Tax-free |
| RMDs during lifetime | Yes (starting at 73) | No |
| Contribution age limit | None | None |

### Contribution Limits (2025)

Same as Traditional IRA:
- Under 50: $7,000
- 50 or older: $8,000

**Note:** The limit is the total for ALL your IRAs (traditional and Roth combined).

### Income Limits for Contributions (2025)

| Filing Status | Full Contribution | Reduced | No Contribution |
|--------------|-------------------|---------|-----------------|
| MFJ/QSS | MAGI < $236,000 | $236,000-$246,000 | MAGI ≥ $246,000 |
| Single/HOH | MAGI < $150,000 | $150,000-$165,000 | MAGI ≥ $165,000 |
| MFS (lived with spouse) | N/A | $0-$10,000 | MAGI ≥ $10,000 |

### Qualified Distributions

A distribution is **qualified** (tax-free and penalty-free) if:
1. Made after the 5-year holding period, **AND**
2. Made after age 59½, OR
   - Due to disability, OR
   - To a beneficiary after your death, OR
   - Up to $10,000 for first home purchase

### Nonqualified Distributions

**Ordering rules for nonqualified distributions:**
1. Regular contributions (always tax-free, penalty-free)
2. Conversion contributions (tax-free; 10% penalty may apply if within 5 years)
3. Earnings (taxable; 10% penalty may apply)

## IRA Rollovers

### What Is a Rollover?

A rollover is a tax-free transfer of assets from one retirement plan to another.

### Direct vs. Indirect Rollovers

| Type | Process | Withholding |
|------|---------|-------------|
| **Direct (trustee-to-trustee)** | Funds transferred directly between custodians | None |
| **Indirect (60-day)** | You receive funds and deposit within 60 days | 20% on qualified plans |

### 60-Day Rule

If you receive a distribution:
- You have **60 days** to roll it over
- One rollover per 12-month period (for indirect rollovers between IRAs)
- The 20% withholding counts toward the rollover amount

### Eligible Rollover Sources

| From | To Traditional IRA | To Roth IRA |
|------|-------------------|-------------|
| Traditional IRA | Yes | Yes (taxable conversion) |
| 401(k)/403(b) | Yes | Yes (taxable conversion) |
| Roth IRA | No | Yes |
| Roth 401(k) | No | Yes |

### Roth Conversions

Converting from traditional IRA to Roth IRA:
- Include converted amount in income (except nondeductible portion)
- No 10% early withdrawal penalty on conversion
- 5-year holding period starts for each conversion

## Required Minimum Distributions

### When RMDs Begin

| Birth Year | RMD Starting Age |
|------------|-----------------|
| Before 1951 | 70½ |
| 1951-1959 | 73 |
| 1960 or later | 75 |

### First RMD Deadline

Your first RMD must be taken by **April 1** of the year following the year you reach RMD age.

> **Note:** If you delay your first RMD to the following year, you must take TWO distributions that year.

### Calculating RMDs

RMD = Account balance (December 31 of prior year) ÷ Life expectancy factor

Use the **Uniform Lifetime Table** unless your sole beneficiary is a spouse more than 10 years younger.

### Roth IRA RMDs

- **During your lifetime:** No RMDs required
- **After your death:** Beneficiaries must take distributions

### Penalty for Missed RMDs

**25% excise tax** on the amount not distributed (reduced to 10% if corrected within 2 years).

## Early Withdrawal Penalties

### 10% Early Withdrawal Penalty

Generally applies to distributions before age 59½.

### Exceptions to 10% Penalty

| Exception | Traditional IRA | Roth IRA |
|-----------|----------------|----------|
| Death | Yes | Yes |
| Disability | Yes | Yes |
| Substantially equal periodic payments (72(t)) | Yes | Yes |
| Medical expenses exceeding 7.5% of AGI | Yes | Yes |
| Health insurance premiums while unemployed | Yes | Yes |
| Qualified higher education expenses | Yes | Yes |
| First-time home purchase (up to $10,000 lifetime) | Yes | Yes |
| IRS levy | Yes | Yes |
| Qualified reservist distribution | Yes | Yes |
| Birth or adoption (up to $5,000) | Yes | Yes |
| Domestic abuse victim (up to $10,000) | Yes | Yes |
| Terminal illness | Yes | Yes |
| Disaster distributions | Yes | Yes |

### Exception Details

**Substantially Equal Periodic Payments (SEPP):**
- Must continue for 5 years or until age 59½, whichever is longer
- Modification triggers penalty on all prior distributions

**First-Time Home Purchase:**
- $10,000 lifetime limit
- For yourself, spouse, child, grandchild, or parent
- "First-time" = no ownership in past 2 years

**Higher Education Expenses:**
- Tuition, fees, books, supplies, equipment
- Room and board (if at least half-time student)
- For you, spouse, children, or grandchildren

## Trump Account (New for 2025)

### What Is a Trump Account?

A new type of IRA for children, established by authorized individuals (parents, guardians).

### Eligibility

Child must be:
- Born after 2024 and before 2029
- A U.S. citizen
- Meet other requirements specified in the law

### Contribution Limits

- **Annual limit:** $5,000 per year
- **Pilot program contribution:** $1,000 (one-time, if elected)

### How to Establish

File **Form 4547** with your tax return to:
- Elect to establish a Trump account
- Elect to receive the $1,000 pilot program contribution

### Tax Treatment

- Contributions: Not deductible
- Growth: Tax-deferred
- Qualified distributions: Tax-free

See Form 4547 instructions for complete details.

## Reporting IRA Transactions

### Form 5498

Your IRA custodian sends Form 5498 showing:
- Contributions made during the year
- Rollover contributions
- Fair market value of account
- RMD amount (if applicable)

### Form 1099-R

Reports distributions:
- **Box 1:** Gross distribution
- **Box 2a:** Taxable amount
- **Box 7:** Distribution code

### Form 8606

File Form 8606 if you:
- Made nondeductible traditional IRA contributions
- Took distributions from traditional IRA with basis
- Converted traditional IRA to Roth
- Received Roth IRA distributions
