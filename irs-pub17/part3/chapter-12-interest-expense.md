## Introduction

Interest is the cost of borrowing money. Certain interest expenses can be deducted, either as an itemized deduction or on other schedules depending on the type of expense.

---

## Home Mortgage Interest

### What Qualifies

**Home mortgage interest** is interest you pay on a loan secured by your:
- Main home, **and/or**
- Second home

The loan must be secured by the home (recorded mortgage or deed of trust).

### Qualified Homes

**Main Home:** Where you live most of the time

**Second Home:** Must be used personally
- If rented out, must be used personally for more than 14 days or 10% of rental days

### Mortgage Debt Limits

| When Mortgage Originated | Debt Limit for Interest Deduction |
|-------------------------|----------------------------------|
| December 16, 2017 or later | $750,000 ($375,000 MFS) |
| Before December 16, 2017 | $1,000,000 ($500,000 MFS) |

**Grandfathered debt:** If you had a mortgage before December 16, 2017, the $1 million limit applies to that debt.

### Home Acquisition Debt

Interest is deductible on mortgage debt used to:
- Buy your home
- Build your home
- Substantially improve your home

### Home Equity Debt

Interest on home equity loans/lines of credit is deductible **only if** the proceeds are used to buy, build, or substantially improve your home.

**Not Deductible:**
- Home equity debt used to pay off credit cards
- Home equity debt used for vacations or other personal expenses
- Home equity debt used to buy a car

### Form 1098

Your lender sends Form 1098 showing:
- **Box 1:** Mortgage interest received
- **Box 2:** Outstanding mortgage principal
- **Box 5:** Mortgage insurance premiums
- **Box 6:** Points paid on purchase

## Mortgage Insurance Premiums

### Deductibility

Qualified mortgage insurance premiums may be deductible as home mortgage interest.

### What Qualifies

- FHA mortgage insurance
- VA funding fee
- USDA guarantee fee
- Private mortgage insurance (PMI)

### Phase-Out

The deduction is reduced by 10% for each $1,000 (or fraction thereof) that your AGI exceeds:
- $100,000 ($50,000 MFS)

No deduction if AGI exceeds:
- $109,000 ($54,500 MFS)

### How to Deduct

Report on Schedule A, line 8d.

## Points

### What Are Points?

Points (also called loan origination fees or maximum loan charges) are prepaid interest.

### Points on Home Purchase

Points paid on a loan to **buy or build** your main home are generally **fully deductible in the year paid** if:
1. The loan is secured by your main home
2. Paying points is an established practice in your area
3. Points aren't more than generally charged in your area
4. You use the cash method of accounting
5. Points weren't paid for items that are usually stated separately (appraisal, inspection, etc.)
6. You provided funds at least equal to the points (down payment, earnest money)
7. Points are computed as a percentage of principal
8. Points are clearly shown on settlement statement

### Points on Refinancing

Points paid to refinance a mortgage are generally **amortized** (deducted ratably) over the life of the loan.

**Exception:** If part of the proceeds is used to improve your main home, you can deduct that portion in the year paid.

### Points Paid by Seller

If the seller pays points for you:
- You can deduct them as if you paid them
- Reduce your home's cost basis by the amount

## Investment Interest

### What Is Investment Interest?

Interest paid on money borrowed to buy property held for investment (stocks, bonds, land).

### Deduction Limit

You can deduct investment interest only up to your **net investment income**.

**Net Investment Income:**
- Interest income
- Ordinary dividends
- Short-term capital gains
- Royalties from investments
- Net passive income from rentals (in some cases)

**NOT included (unless you elect):**
- Qualified dividends
- Long-term capital gains

### Carryover

Investment interest that exceeds net investment income can be carried forward to future years.

### Form 4952

Use Form 4952 to calculate your investment interest expense deduction.

### Example

```
Investment interest paid:                 $5,000
Net investment income:                    $3,000

Deductible in current year:               $3,000
Carried forward:                          $2,000
```

## Car Loan Interest (New for 2025)

### New Deduction

Beginning in 2025, you may be able to deduct **qualified passenger vehicle loan interest** on vehicles purchased for personal use.

### Requirements

| Requirement | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| Vehicle purchased in | 2025 |
| Use | Personal (not business) |
| Maximum deduction | $10,000 |
| MAGI limit | Phase-out begins at $100,000 ($200,000 MFJ) |

### What Qualifies

- Interest on a loan to purchase a passenger vehicle
- Vehicle must be for personal use
- Loan must be taken in connection with 2025 purchase

### What Doesn't Qualify

- Leased vehicles (lease payments are not interest)
- Business vehicles (deduct on Schedule C instead)
- Vehicles purchased before 2025

### How to Claim

Report on **Schedule 1-A (Form 1040)**.

### Phase-Out Calculation

The deduction is reduced if MAGI exceeds:
- $100,000 (Single, HOH)
- $200,000 (MFJ)

## Interest You Cannot Deduct

### Personal Interest

You cannot deduct personal interest, including:
- Credit card interest
- Car loan interest (except new 2025 deduction)
- Personal loan interest
- Interest on life insurance loans
- Late payment charges on utilities
- Interest on tax underpayments (for individuals)

### Other Non-Deductible Interest

| Type | Reason |
|------|--------|
| Interest to purchase tax-exempt securities | Not deductible |
| Service charges on installment purchases | Not interest |
| Interest paid with borrowed funds | Complex rules apply |
| Prepaid interest (beyond current year) | Deduct when it accrues |
| Interest on debt used to buy market discount bonds | May reduce deduction |

### Student Loan Interest

Student loan interest is deductible as an **adjustment to income** (Schedule 1), not as an itemized deduction.

- Maximum deduction: $2,500
- Phase-out based on income
- Available even if you don't itemize

---

## Allocation of Interest

### Mixed-Use Loans

If loan proceeds are used for more than one purpose, you must **allocate the interest** based on use:

**Example:**
$100,000 home equity loan:
- $60,000 used for home improvement → Mortgage interest
- $40,000 used to buy stocks → Investment interest

Interest allocation: 60% mortgage interest, 40% investment interest

### Tracing Rules

Interest is allocated based on how the loan proceeds are actually used, not the security for the loan.

## How to Report Interest Deductions

| Type of Interest | Where to Report |
|------------------|-----------------|
| Home mortgage interest | Schedule A, line 8a |
| Mortgage interest (not on 1098) | Schedule A, line 8b |
| Points (not on 1098) | Schedule A, line 8c |
| Mortgage insurance premiums | Schedule A, line 8d |
| Investment interest | Schedule A, line 9 (with Form 4952) |
| Car loan interest (2025) | Schedule 1-A |
| Student loan interest | Schedule 1, line 21 |
| Business interest | Schedule C, E, or F |
