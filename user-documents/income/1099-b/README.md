# 1099-B (Proceeds from Broker Transactions)

Place your 1099-B forms here.

## What Is a 1099-B?

Brokerages send you a 1099-B when you **sell** stocks, bonds, mutual funds, or other securities.

## What to Look For

| Box | Description | Notes |
|-----|-------------|-------|
| 1a | Description of property | What you sold |
| 1b | Date acquired | When you bought it |
| 1c | Date sold | When you sold it |
| 1d | Proceeds | Sale price |
| 1e | Cost or other basis | What you paid (may be blank) |
| 1g | Adjustments | Wash sales, etc. |

## Short-Term vs. Long-Term

- **Short-term:** Held 1 year or less → Taxed as ordinary income
- **Long-term:** Held more than 1 year → Lower tax rates (0%, 15%, 20%)

## Basis Reporting

Your broker may or may not report your cost basis:
- **Covered securities:** Basis reported to IRS
- **Noncovered securities:** You must determine and report basis

## Supplemental Information

Many brokers provide detailed transaction summaries. Include these—they're often more helpful than the 1099-B itself.

## Crypto/Digital Assets

Starting in 2025, crypto sales may also appear on 1099-B or the new 1099-DA.
