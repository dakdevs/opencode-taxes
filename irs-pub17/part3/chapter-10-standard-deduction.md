## Introduction

Most taxpayers have a choice of either taking a **standard deduction** or **itemizing deductions** on Schedule A.

**The standard deduction:**
- Is a dollar amount that reduces your taxable income
- Varies based on your filing status
- May be increased if you're 65 or older or blind
- Eliminates the need to keep records of actual expenses

---

## Standard Deduction Amounts

### 2025 Standard Deduction by Filing Status

| Filing Status | Standard Deduction |
|--------------|-------------------|
| Single | $15,750 |
| Married Filing Jointly | $31,500 |
| Married Filing Separately | $15,750 |
| Head of Household | $23,625 |
| Qualifying Surviving Spouse | $31,500 |

---

## Additional Standard Deduction

### Who Qualifies

You get an additional standard deduction if you are:
- **65 or older** (born before January 2, 1961), **or**
- **Blind** at the end of the tax year

### 2025 Additional Amounts

| Filing Status | Age 65+ OR Blind | Age 65+ AND Blind |
|--------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Single or HOH | $2,000 | $4,000 |
| MFJ, MFS, or QSS | $1,600 (each spouse) | $3,200 (each spouse) |

### Combined Standard Deduction Examples

**Example 1:** Single person, age 67
- Basic: $15,750
- Additional (65+): $2,000
- **Total: $17,750**

**Example 2:** Married filing jointly, both age 70
- Basic: $31,500
- Additional (65+ for each): $1,600 × 2 = $3,200
- **Total: $34,700**

**Example 3:** Single person, age 68, blind
- Basic: $15,750
- Additional (65+): $2,000
- Additional (blind): $2,000
- **Total: $19,750**

### Definition of Blindness

You are considered blind if:
- You cannot see better than 20/200 in the better eye with glasses or contact lenses, **or**
- Your field of vision is 20 degrees or less

You must have a certified statement from your eye doctor.

## Who Should Itemize

### Compare to Decide

You should itemize deductions if your total itemized deductions are **greater than** your standard deduction.

### Common Itemized Deductions

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **Medical expenses** | Doctor visits, prescriptions, insurance premiums (exceeding 7.5% of AGI) |
| **Taxes** | State and local income tax, property tax (up to $40,000 limit) |
| **Interest** | Home mortgage interest, investment interest |
| **Charitable contributions** | Cash, property, mileage for charity work |
| **Casualty/theft losses** | Federally declared disaster losses only |

### When Itemizing Usually Makes Sense

Consider itemizing if you:
- Paid significant mortgage interest
- Paid high state and local taxes
- Made large charitable contributions
- Had significant unreimbursed medical expenses
- Had casualty losses from a federally declared disaster

---

## Who Must Itemize

You **must itemize** and cannot take the standard deduction if:

1. **Married filing separately** and your spouse itemizes deductions

2. **Nonresident alien** or dual-status alien during the year (exceptions exist)

3. **Short tax year** due to a change in accounting period

4. **Estate or trust** (generally)

## Dependents

### Limitation on Standard Deduction

If you can be claimed as a dependent on someone else's return, your standard deduction is **limited**.

### Calculating Dependent's Standard Deduction (2025)

The standard deduction for a dependent is the **greater of:**
1. $1,350, **or**
2. Earned income plus $450 (but not more than the regular standard deduction)

### Examples

**Example 1:** Dependent with no earned income
- Greater of $1,350 or ($0 + $450 = $450)
- Standard deduction: **$1,350**

**Example 2:** Dependent with $2,000 earned income (single)
- Greater of $1,350 or ($2,000 + $450 = $2,450)
- Standard deduction: **$2,450**

**Example 3:** Dependent with $18,000 earned income (single)
- Greater of $1,350 or ($18,000 + $450 = $18,450)
- But limited to regular standard deduction
- Standard deduction: **$15,750** (capped at single standard deduction)

### Additional Standard Deduction for Dependents

A dependent who is 65 or older or blind can add the additional standard deduction amounts to their limited standard deduction.

## New Deductions for 2025

### Schedule 1-A Deductions

Beginning in 2025, four new deductions are available regardless of whether you take the standard deduction or itemize:

### 1. No Tax on Tips

| Detail | Amount/Limit |
|--------|--------------|
| Maximum deduction | $25,000 |
| MAGI phase-out begins | $150,000 ($300,000 MFJ) |
| SSN required | Yes |
| Must file jointly if married | Yes |

### 2. No Tax on Overtime

| Detail | Amount/Limit |
|--------|--------------|
| Maximum deduction | $12,500 ($25,000 MFJ) |
| MAGI phase-out begins | $150,000 ($300,000 MFJ) |
| SSN required | Yes |
| Must file jointly if married | Yes |

### 3. No Tax on Car Loan Interest

| Detail | Amount/Limit |
|--------|--------------|
| Maximum deduction | $10,000 |
| MAGI phase-out begins | $100,000 ($200,000 MFJ) |
| Applies to | Vehicles purchased in 2025 for personal use |

### 4. Enhanced Senior Deduction

| Detail | Amount/Limit |
|--------|--------------|
| Maximum deduction | $6,000 ($12,000 if both eligible) |
| Eligibility | Born before January 2, 1961 |
| MAGI phase-out begins | $75,000 ($150,000 MFJ) |
| SSN required | Yes |
| Must file jointly if married | Yes |

### How to Claim

Report these deductions on **Schedule 1-A (Form 1040)**.

## Standard Deduction vs. Itemizing Worksheet

Use this worksheet to compare:

```
ITEMIZED DEDUCTIONS:
Medical expenses (over 7.5% of AGI)    $________
State and local taxes (up to $40,000)  $________
Mortgage interest                       $________
Charitable contributions                $________
Casualty losses (disaster only)         $________
Other itemized deductions               $________
                                        ─────────
TOTAL ITEMIZED DEDUCTIONS              $________  (A)

STANDARD DEDUCTION:
Basic amount for filing status         $________
Additional (65+, blind)                $________
                                        ─────────
TOTAL STANDARD DEDUCTION               $________  (B)

COMPARE:
If (A) > (B): ITEMIZE
If (B) ≥ (A): TAKE STANDARD DEDUCTION
```

## Special Situations

### Disaster Losses

If you had a net qualified disaster loss, you can claim:
- Your itemized deductions, **PLUS**
- An increased standard deduction for the disaster loss

### Death of Spouse

If your spouse died during the year, you may be able to use the MFJ standard deduction if you file a joint return.

### Real Estate Professionals

Real estate professionals can still only deduct up to $40,000 in state and local taxes like all other taxpayers.

## Summary: Choosing Standard vs. Itemized

| Choose Standard Deduction If... | Choose Itemizing If... |
|--------------------------------|------------------------|
| Your itemized deductions are less than standard | Your itemized deductions exceed standard |
| You don't want to keep records | You have significant medical expenses |
| Your tax situation is simple | You paid high mortgage interest |
| You're single with minimal deductions | You made large charitable contributions |
| | You paid high state/local taxes |
