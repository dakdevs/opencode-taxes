## Introduction

The federal income tax is a pay-as-you-go tax. You must pay the tax as you earn or receive income during the year. There are two ways to pay:

1. **Withholding:** Tax is taken from your pay, pension, or other income
2. **Estimated Tax:** You make quarterly payments directly to the IRS

If you don't pay enough tax through withholding or estimated tax payments, you may be charged a penalty.

---

## Tax Withholding

### Types of Income Subject to Withholding

**Mandatory Withholding:**
- Wages, salaries, bonuses, commissions
- Tips (if reported to employer)
- Taxable fringe benefits

**Optional Withholding (upon request):**
- Pensions and annuities
- Gambling winnings
- Unemployment compensation
- Social Security benefits
- IRA distributions

**Not Subject to Withholding:**
- Interest and dividends (unless backup withholding applies)
- Self-employment income
- Rental income
- Alimony
- Capital gains

### How Withholding Is Calculated

Your employer calculates withholding based on:
- The information you provide on Form W-4
- The amount of your wages
- Your pay frequency
- IRS withholding tables

## Form W-4

### When To Submit Form W-4

Submit a new Form W-4 when:
- You start a new job
- Your personal or financial situation changes
- You want to change your withholding amount

### What's on Form W-4

The current Form W-4 asks for:

**Step 1: Personal Information**
- Your name, address, SSN
- Filing status (Single, Married Filing Jointly, Head of Household)

**Step 2: Multiple Jobs or Spouse Works**
Complete if:
- You hold more than one job at a time, **or**
- You're married filing jointly and your spouse also works

Options:
- Use the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator
- Use the Multiple Jobs Worksheet
- Check the box in Step 2(c) for roughly equal-paying jobs

**Step 3: Claim Dependents**
- Multiply qualifying children under 17 by $2,000
- Multiply other dependents by $500
- Add amounts and enter total

**Step 4: Other Adjustments (Optional)**
- (a) Other income not from jobs
- (b) Deductions (if you plan to claim more than standard deduction)
- (c) Extra withholding per pay period

### Tips for Accurate Withholding

1. **Use the Tax Withholding Estimator** at [IRS.gov/W4App](https://www.irs.gov/w4app)

2. **Review your withholding** after major life events:
   - Marriage or divorce
   - Birth or adoption of a child
   - Buying a home
   - Starting or losing a job
   - Change in income

3. **Check your withholding mid-year** to avoid surprises at tax time

### Exemption From Withholding

You may claim exemption from withholding if:
- You had no federal income tax liability last year, **AND**
- You expect no federal income tax liability this year

**To claim exemption:** Write "Exempt" in the space below Step 4(c) on Form W-4.

> **Note:** You must submit a new Form W-4 by February 15 each year to maintain exempt status.

## Estimated Tax

### Who Must Pay Estimated Tax

You may need to pay estimated tax if:
- You expect to owe at least $1,000 in tax after subtracting withholding and refundable credits
- Your withholding and refundable credits will be less than the smaller of:
  - 90% of the tax shown on your 2025 return, **or**
  - 100% of the tax shown on your 2024 return (110% if 2024 AGI was over $150,000)

### Who Typically Pays Estimated Tax

- Self-employed individuals
- Sole proprietors and partners
- S corporation shareholders
- Landlords with rental income
- Those with significant investment income
- Retirees without adequate withholding

### Payment Due Dates (2025 Tax Year)

| Payment Period | Due Date |
|---------------|----------|
| January 1 - March 31 | April 15, 2025 |
| April 1 - May 31 | June 16, 2025 |
| June 1 - August 31 | September 15, 2025 |
| September 1 - December 31 | January 15, 2026 |

> **Note:** If a due date falls on a weekend or holiday, it moves to the next business day.

### How To Calculate Estimated Tax

Use **Form 1040-ES** to calculate your estimated tax.

**Basic calculation:**
1. Estimate your AGI for the year
2. Subtract deductions (standard or itemized)
3. Calculate tax using tax tables or rates
4. Subtract credits
5. Subtract expected withholding
6. Divide by 4 (or remaining payments)

### How To Pay Estimated Tax

**Online:**
- IRS Direct Pay at [IRS.gov/DirectPay](https://www.irs.gov/directpay)
- EFTPS at [EFTPS.gov](https://www.eftps.gov)
- Credit or debit card

**By Mail:**
- Send payment with Form 1040-ES voucher
- Mail to the address for your state listed in Form 1040-ES instructions

### Safe Harbor Rules

You won't owe a penalty if your payments equal or exceed:

| 2024 AGI | Safe Harbor |
|----------|-------------|
| $150,000 or less ($75,000 MFS) | 100% of 2024 tax |
| Over $150,000 ($75,000 MFS) | 110% of 2024 tax |

**OR** you pay at least 90% of your 2025 tax.

## Penalties

### Underpayment Penalty

You may owe a penalty if:
- You didn't pay enough tax during the year through withholding or estimated payments
- You paid estimated tax late

### Penalty Calculation

The penalty is calculated on **Form 2210** based on:
- Amount of underpayment
- Period of underpayment
- Interest rate for underpayments

### Avoiding the Penalty

You won't owe a penalty if:
- You owe less than $1,000 with your return
- You paid at least 90% of your 2025 tax, **or** 100% of your 2024 tax (110% if 2024 AGI over $150,000)
- You're a farmer or fisherman and follow special rules

### Penalty Exceptions

The IRS may waive the penalty if:
- The underpayment was due to casualty, disaster, or other unusual circumstances
- You retired after reaching age 62 or became disabled during the year
- The underpayment was due to reasonable cause and not willful neglect

## Adjusting Withholding vs. Estimated Payments

| Situation | Best Approach |
|-----------|---------------|
| Steady W-2 job | Adjust Form W-4 |
| Self-employed | Estimated tax payments |
| Mix of W-2 and 1099 | Can use either or both |
| Variable income | Estimated payments may be easier to adjust |
| Want larger refund | Increase withholding or payments |
| Want smaller refund | Decrease withholding (don't underpay!) |

## Special Situations

### Household Employers

If you pay a household employee (nanny, housekeeper, etc.) $2,700 or more in 2025:
- You must withhold and pay Social Security and Medicare taxes
- You may need to withhold income tax if employee requests
- Report on Schedule H with your Form 1040

### Backup Withholding

Banks and other payers may withhold 24% from interest, dividends, and other payments if:
- You don't provide your correct TIN
- You underreported interest or dividends
- The IRS notifies the payer to begin backup withholding

### Foreign Persons

Special withholding rules apply to payments made to foreign persons. Generally, 30% is withheld unless a tax treaty provides a lower rate.

## Summary

| Method | Best For | Due Dates |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| **Withholding** | Employees with steady paychecks | Each paycheck |
| **Estimated Tax** | Self-employed, investment income | Apr 15, Jun 16, Sep 15, Jan 15 |
| **Both** | Mixed income sources | Varies |

**Key Numbers for 2025:**
- Penalty threshold: $1,000 owed
- Safe harbor: 90% of current year or 100%/110% of prior year
- Backup withholding rate: 24%
- Household employee threshold: $2,700
