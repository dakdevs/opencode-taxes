# What's New for 2025

[< Back to Index](../README.md)

---

This section summarizes important tax changes that took effect in 2025. Most of these changes are discussed in more detail throughout this publication.

> **Future developments.** For the latest information about the tax law topics covered in this publication, such as legislation enacted after it was published, go to [IRS.gov/Pub17](https://www.irs.gov/pub17).

## Filing Requirements

### Who Must File
Generally, the amount of income you can receive before you must file a return has been increased. For more information, see [Chapter 1: Filing Information](../part1/chapter-01-filing-information.md).

### Due Date of Return
File Form 1040 or 1040-SR by **April 15, 2026**. See [Chapter 1](../part1/chapter-01-filing-information.md).

## New Tax Accounts and Forms

### Trump Account and New Form 4547
Recent legislation allows parents, guardians, and other authorized individuals to elect to establish a new type of individual retirement account, called a **Trump account**, for the exclusive benefit of certain children.

**Eligibility:**
- Child was born after 2024 and before 2029
- Child is a U.S. citizen
- Meets certain other requirements

**Pilot Program Contribution:** The authorized individual may also elect to receive a **$1,000 pilot program contribution** to the child's Trump account.

Both elections can be made on **Form 4547**, which can be filed at the same time as the authorized individual's 2025 income tax return.

## Standard Deduction

The standard deduction amount has been increased for all filers:

| Filing Status | 2025 Amount |
|--------------|-------------|
| Single or Married filing separately | $15,750 |
| Married filing jointly or Qualifying surviving spouse | $31,500 |
| Head of household | $23,625 |

See [Chapter 10: Standard Deduction](../part3/chapter-10-standard-deduction.md).

## Retirement Contributions

### Higher Catch-Up Contribution Limit (Ages 60-63)
If, at the end of 2025, you were at least age 60, but younger than age 64, and you participated in a deferred compensation plan (including most 401(k), 403(b), and governmental 457 plans, and the governmental Thrift Savings Plan), a higher catch-up contribution limit may apply.

**2025 Higher Catch-Up Limit:** $11,250

### Deferred Compensation Contribution Limits
If you participate in a 401(k) plan, 403(b) plan, or the federal government's Thrift Savings Plan, the total annual amount you can contribute for 2025 is:

| Age Category | 2025 Limit |
|--------------|------------|
| Under 50 | $23,500 |
| Age 50 or older | $31,000 |
| Age 60, 61, 62, or 63 | $37,750 |

This also applies to most section 457 plans.

## New Deductions (Schedule 1-A)

Recent legislation provided for **four new deductions** that take effect beginning in 2025. If you are eligible, you can claim these deductions whether you take the standard deduction or itemize on Schedule A.

### 1. No Tax on Tips
- **Maximum deduction:** $25,000 of qualified tips
- **Income limit:** Deduction is limited if MAGI exceeds $150,000 ($300,000 MFJ)
- **Requirements:**
  - You and/or your spouse who received tips must have a valid SSN
  - If married, you must file a joint return

### 2. No Tax on Overtime
- **Maximum deduction:** $12,500 ($25,000 MFJ) of qualified overtime compensation
- **Income limit:** Deduction is limited if MAGI exceeds $150,000 ($300,000 MFJ)
- **Requirements:**
  - You and/or your spouse who received overtime must have a valid SSN
  - If married, you must file a joint return

### 3. No Tax on Car Loan Interest
- **Maximum deduction:** $10,000 of qualified passenger vehicle loan interest
- **Applicable to:** Vehicles purchased in 2025 for personal use
- **Income limit:** Deduction is limited if MAGI exceeds $100,000 ($200,000 MFJ)

### 4. Enhanced Deduction for Seniors
- **Eligibility:** Born before January 2, 1961
- **Maximum deduction:** $6,000 ($12,000 if both spouses eligible)
- **Income limit:** Deduction is limited if MAGI exceeds $75,000 ($150,000 MFJ)
- **Requirements:**
  - You and/or your spouse must have a valid SSN
  - If married, you must file a joint return

## State and Local Tax (SALT) Deduction

The overall limit on the deduction for state and local income, sales, and property taxes has **increased to $40,000** ($20,000 if married filing separately).

**Phase-out:** The limit is reduced if your modified AGI is more than $500,000 ($250,000 MFS), but will not be reduced below $10,000 ($5,000 MFS).

## Form Changes

### Main Home in the United States
If your main home (and spouse if filing a joint return) was in the United States for over half of 2025, check the box on the front of Form 1040 or 1040-SR. This helps the IRS determine your eligibility for certain tax benefits, including the earned income credit.

### Changes to the Dependents Section
The Dependents section now has:
- Numbered rows
- Additional questions about you and your dependents

This information helps the IRS determine your eligibility for:
- Child tax credit
- Credit for other dependents
- Earned income credit

### Write-In Information
Beginning in 2025, most words, codes, and dollar amounts that explain income or deduction items now have a dedicated checkbox or entry space (instead of writing next to specific lines).

### Death of a Taxpayer
If filing for someone who died before filing a 2025 return:
- Check the "Deceased" box at the top of Form 1040 or 1040-SR
- Enter the date of death

### New Schedule 1-A
Created for taxpayers to claim deductions for:
- No tax on tips
- No tax on overtime
- No tax on car loan interest
- Enhanced senior deduction

## Digital Assets

### Form 1099-DA
If you used a broker to sell a digital asset in 2025, your broker should send you a **Form 1099-DA** reporting the transaction information.

**Note:** In 2025, brokers have the option (but are not required) to report your basis in the digital asset.

**Important:** Even if you receive a Form 1099-DA, you must still answer the digital assets question on page 1 of Form 1040 or 1040-SR.

## Payments and Refunds

### Electronic Payments and Direct Deposit
The IRS recommends:
- Using **direct deposit** for any refunds (if you have access to U.S. banking services)
- Paying **electronically** whenever possible

Payment options include:
- Bank account with Direct Pay
- Debit or credit card
- Digital wallet
- Online account

Visit [IRS.gov/Payments](https://www.irs.gov/payments) for all options.

### Delayed Refund for ACTC Claims
The IRS cannot issue refunds before **mid-February 2026** for returns that properly claim the Additional Child Tax Credit (ACTC). This applies to the entire refund, not just the ACTC portion.

## Credits

### Credit for Other Dependents
Beginning in 2025, to claim this credit you or your spouse (if filing jointly) must have a valid SSN or ITIN issued before the return due date (including extensions).

### Changes to Adoption Credit
**Refundable portion:** Up to $5,000 of your adoption credit may now be refundable, determined separately for each eligible child.

**Tribal Government Parity:** State and Indian Tribal government determinations of special needs are now both recognized for adoption credit purposes.

**2025 Amounts:**
- Adoption credit: $17,280 per eligible child
- Exclusion for employer-provided adoption benefits: $17,280 per eligible child
- Phase-out begins: Modified AGI over $259,190
- Complete phase-out: Modified AGI of $299,190 or more

## IRA Contribution Limits

### Traditional IRA Deduction Phase-Out (If Covered by Retirement Plan at Work)

| Filing Status | Phase-Out Range |
|--------------|-----------------|
| MFJ or Qualifying surviving spouse | $126,000 - $146,000 |
| Single or Head of household | $79,000 - $89,000 |
| MFS | Less than $10,000 |

**If spouse is covered but you aren't:** Phase-out is $236,000 - $246,000. No deduction if MAGI is $246,000 or more.

### Roth IRA Contribution Phase-Out

| Filing Status | Phase-Out Range |
|--------------|-----------------|
| MFJ or Qualifying surviving spouse | $236,000 - $246,000 |
| Single, HOH, or MFS (didn't live with spouse) | $150,000 - $165,000 |
| MFS (lived with spouse) | $0 - $10,000 |

## Other Changes

### Contributions to Governmental Paid Family Leave Program
Beginning in 2025:
- Include the full amount of contributions in your income
- If you itemize, include amounts contributed as part of state and local income taxes paid

### Deferral of Gain in Qualified Opportunity Fund (QOF)
Taxpayers meeting the 7-year holding period threshold may be eligible for a basis adjustment. See Form 8997 instructions.

### Election for Farmland Sale
If your tax year began after July 4, 2025, and you sold farmland to a qualified farmer after that date, you can elect to pay the net income tax in **four equal installments**. See Form 1062.

### Domestic Research and Experimental Expenditures
Beginning in 2025, taxpayers can elect to:
- Charge domestic R&E expenditures to a capital account (rather than deducting)
- Take amortization deduction over not less than 60 months
- Elect to deduct certain unamortized amounts from 2022-2024

See Revenue Procedure 2025-28.

### Health FSA Dollar Limit
For 2025, the limit on voluntary salary reductions for health FSAs is **$3,300**.

### Standard Mileage Rates (2025)

| Purpose | Rate |
|---------|------|
| Business use | 70 cents/mile |
| Charitable volunteer work | 14 cents/mile |
| Medical reasons | 21 cents/mile |

### Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT)

| Filing Status | 2025 Exemption |
|--------------|----------------|
| Single | $88,100 |
| MFJ or Qualifying surviving spouse | $137,000 |
| MFS | $68,500 |

**Phase-out begins:** $626,350 ($1,252,700 MFJ)

### Form 1099-K Reporting Threshold
Payment card companies, payment apps, and online marketplaces send Form 1099-K only if:
- Business transactions exceed $20,000, **AND**
- Total transactions exceed 200

### TAC Appointment Notifications
Beginning in 2025, taxpayers with scheduled Taxpayer Assistance Center appointments may receive confirmations, reminders, and cancellation notices via text message.
