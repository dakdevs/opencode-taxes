# 2025 Qualified Business Income (QBI) Deduction

## Overview

Section 199A allows a deduction of up to **20%** of qualified business income from pass-through entities (sole proprietorships, partnerships, S corporations, trusts, estates).

---

## 2025 Income Thresholds

| Filing Status | Full Deduction | Phase-Out Range | No Deduction |
|---------------|----------------|-----------------|--------------|
| Single | ≤ $197,300 | $197,301 - $247,300 | > $247,300 |
| Married Filing Jointly | ≤ $394,600 | $394,601 - $494,600 | > $494,600 |
| Married Filing Separately | ≤ $197,300 | $197,301 - $247,300 | > $247,300 |
| Head of Household | ≤ $197,300 | $197,301 - $247,300 | > $247,300 |

---

## Basic Calculation (Below Threshold)

If taxable income is **at or below** the threshold:

```
QBI Deduction = Lesser of:
  (A) 20% of Qualified Business Income, OR
  (B) 20% of (Taxable Income - Net Capital Gains)
```

## What is Qualified Business Income?

**Included**:
- Net income from trade or business
- Ordinary gains/losses from business property
- Section 1231 gains/losses

**Excluded**:
- W-2 wages paid to yourself
- Guaranteed payments (partnerships)
- Investment income (interest, dividends, capital gains)
- Reasonable compensation from S corp
- Income from SSTBs (above threshold)

---

## Specified Service Trade or Business (SSTB)

SSTBs face limitations above the income threshold.

### SSTB Categories

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| Health | Doctors, dentists, nurses, physical therapists |
| Law | Attorneys, paralegals |
| Accounting | CPAs, bookkeepers, tax preparers |
| Actuarial science | Actuaries |
| Performing arts | Actors, musicians, athletes |
| Consulting | Management, business consultants |
| Financial services | Investment advisors, brokers |
| Brokerage services | Stock brokers, real estate agents |

### SSTB Phase-Out

| Income Level | SSTB QBI Eligible |
|--------------|-------------------|
| At or below threshold | 100% |
| In phase-out range | Partial (linear reduction) |
| Above phase-out | 0% |

## W-2 Wage and Capital Limitations (Above Threshold)

For non-SSTB businesses above the income threshold, QBI deduction is limited to the **greater of**:

**Limit A**: 50% of W-2 wages paid by the business, OR

**Limit B**: 25% of W-2 wages + 2.5% of unadjusted basis of qualified property

## Aggregation of Businesses

You may aggregate multiple businesses if they meet certain requirements:
- Same tax year
- Common ownership (50% or more)
- Businesses share certain characteristics

Benefits of aggregation:
- Combine W-2 wages across businesses
- Combine qualified property basis
- Offset losses from one business against income from another

---

## REIT/PTP Income

A separate 20% deduction is available for:
- REIT dividends (ordinary, not capital gains)
- Publicly Traded Partnership (PTP) income

This deduction is **not** subject to the W-2 wage or SSTB limitations.

### REIT/PTP Worksheet

```

```

---

## Complete QBI Deduction

```
Total QBI Deduction = 
  QBI component (from business) + REIT/PTP component

Subject to overall limit:
  Cannot exceed 20% of (Taxable Income - Net Capital Gains)
```

## Common Situations

### Sole Proprietor (Schedule C) - Below Threshold
- Simple: 20% of net profit (Schedule C, Line 31)
- No W-2 wage or SSTB limitations

### S Corporation Owner - Above Threshold
- QBI = S corp income minus reasonable compensation
- Subject to W-2 wage limitation
- May benefit from paying higher wages (increases limitation)

### Multiple Businesses - Mixed Results
- Calculate QBI for each business separately
- Consider aggregation if beneficial
- Losses reduce overall QBI

## Forms Required

- **Form 8995**: Simplified QBI Deduction (below threshold)
- **Form 8995-A**: QBI Deduction (above threshold or complex situations)
- **Form 1040**: Line 13 (QBI deduction)

---

## Planning Strategies

1. **Stay below threshold**: Consider timing income/deductions
2. **Increase W-2 wages**: If above threshold, higher wages may increase deduction
3. **Acquire qualified property**: Increases 2.5% component
4. **Aggregate businesses**: Maximize wage/capital limitations
5. **Convert SSTBs**: Restructure if possible to non-SSTB activities
